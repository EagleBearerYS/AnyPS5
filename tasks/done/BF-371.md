## Why

BF-370 (`results/cloud-3/BF-370.md` on the fork's `cloud-results`) built W, a synthetic wave32 shader. It reproduces Marvel's Wolverine (PPSA03671)'s visible chain opcode for opcode:
- a record table from the SRT;
- the record's pointer read at a waterfall key, `ShiftLeftLogical32(ReadFirstLane(SelectU32(LogicalAnd, IAdd32, Phi), Phi(LogicalAnd, LogicalAnd)), Immediate)`;
- a V# through that pointer;
- an S# from that V#.

Its variant **W+S#** (`NestedWaterfallConsumerCode`, 59 words) adds `v_mov_b32 v7, s33` after the sample, so S# word 1 has a second user.

**What happened at #9790's head ebaa9b15651eab3c232e2f30ec323d0735bebdc6, and at #9969's c147aa8647f32558127b91c8afbf4979be094283:**
- Planning accepts W+S#. At #9969, `TryMakeTableColumn`'s `sameHandle` sets `otherUsers`, and the V# counts as rereadable.
- The dispatch then segfaults inside lavapipe's JIT code on four threads: `movzbl (%rcx),%ecx` with `rcx = 0` (BF-370 §6.4).
- W, and W+T# (a T# read through the same V#), plan and pass at both heads.

**#9790's own scalar-keyed case passes.** That's "nested sampler table: an S# with another consumer" in `RunNestedSamplerTests`, `NestedSamplerCode` plus the same `0x7e0e0221`. So the fault needs the waterfall key, or the loop, together with #9790's "a real V# for the other users" path.

#9790 claims that path for T#s and S#s: a word that also feeds another instruction is read through an unrewritten copy of its V# handle, whose word loads stay real BDA loads through the record pointer. A null BDA address is a likely shape. BF-149 found that a missed scalar BDA load segfaults lavapipe. #9790 is ready and approved by XairRrR, so a crash in a path it claims should be found before it merges.
