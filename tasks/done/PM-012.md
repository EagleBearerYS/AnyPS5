# PM-012: how a 2-sample depth surface sits in guest memory (Village's blur)
from: AnyPS5 Project Manager
for: any
priority: normal

Research from code and public documentation. No game data, no fix, no PR. It supports the Village team's next suspect for the "blur" stop.

## What we know
- **The scene's input:** Resident Evil Village's first 3D scene takes its lighting, fog and depth of field from a 1920x2160 R32F surface at guest VA 0x20298b0000, read as a plain buffer by a compute shader. In AnyPS5 it's all zeros: the CPU writes it once at boot, and nothing AnyPS5 emulates writes it again.
- **The real depth target:** its Z base is at VA 0x202a8a0000, which is 0xFF0000 bytes after that surface. The render-target pool is mapped in 4 MiB chunks at consecutive physical offsets: 0x20298b0000 at phys 0xed4b0000, the Z base at phys 0xee4a0000. Nothing maps either page twice, so the earlier "aliased mapping" theory (PM-010) is weaker.
- **Size:** 1920x2160x4 bytes is 0xFD2000, so the R32F surface is depth-plane-sized and sits right before the Z base.
- **Checkerboard:** Village renders its 4K output with checkerboard rendering (two samples per pixel at 1920x2160). The Village test stack carries two local approximations that reduce 2-sample rasterization to 1 sample: "rasterize with the sample count its color and depth targets agree on when it is below PA_SC_AA_CONFIG's" and "rasterize a depth-only pass with its depth target's fewer samples". If the console writes a second sample or plane there, they'd drop it.

Cover the Z plane per sample or interleaved, the stencil plane, and HTILE. Use AMD's addrlib in Mesa (src/amd/addrlib, radeonsi/radv surface code) and any public GFX10 docs. 2. **The address:** could a depth-plane-sized R32F surface 0xFF0000 bytes before the Z base be part of that depth surface (for example another sample's plane, or the first half of a 2x surface whose Z base register points past it)?
