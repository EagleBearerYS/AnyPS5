## Why

At Resident Evil Village's menu, the draw committer spends 1,183 ms per 10 s in `Revalidate` with #12745's index on (a profiled boot of a tree that carries #12745, `APS5_TABLE_INDEX=1`).
- **The index's own proofs:** 173 ms (6,424 proofs, 6,304 accepted, 120 fallbacks, 0 bumps).
- **About 1,010 ms is 1,865 full walks.** 1,869 are forced by #8047's audit (fisherjoey, b4cd843324 on `main`): `APS5_TEMPLATE_AUDIT_MS`, default 1000, sends every cached template to the full walk once a second even after the index proved it. See `if (fast && TemplateAuditDue(lastFullWalk))` in `Graphics/src/ShaderResources.cpp`, ~4064 at #12745's head 6a930fc672.

**Why the audit exists** (b4cd843324's message): a sampled lookup's answer can change with no write. Its two cases:
- a released texture;
- a surface that becomes host-imported and is viewed through its storage image from then on.

**What the index already does:** it bumps a subscription on `Release` and on an `Import` over its blocks (`Execution/src/GuestMemory.cpp` :159, :270, :2074, :2107 at 6a930fc672), with reds in `tests/execution/TableIndexValidation.cpp`.

**A third case nobody bumps:** Sam-mon7's open #11444 (head fc5679f3ea) shows that registering a depth surface at a sampled texture's base, or a multisampled one over it, changes the answer with no write. Today only the audit catches it.

**Neighbours, not alternatives:**
- #9012 (SP4C3B4R-8, open) audits only after the process-wide import set changes;
- #11444 adds a depth refusal to `fastRevalidate` and `fastRevalidateEach`, but not to #12745's `indexRevalidate`.
