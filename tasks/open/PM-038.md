## First guess, now corrected below (see Counters)
- PM-028's memo is per thread, 16 columns and 4 Mi 32-bit words.
- Village's whole tables (per-lane keys) are 65,536-record columns of 8 dwords, over 0.5 Mi words each. With more than about 7 distinct whole-table columns cycling per frame, the memo thrashes.
- Every capture then pays the pre-read stamp, the vouch, the flushes and a multi-MB copy into the memo, and is never reused. The worse `Driver.Draw` time fits cache pollution from those copies.
- **Second possibility:** the tables are written every frame, so the vouch always fails and the memo only adds work.

## Counters from the PC (added when available)
**diagT (cut #3's tree, `APS5_TRACE_IMAGE_TABLE=1`, 5 min at the static menu, 118,049 table lines):**
- 261 distinct (shader, table) columns in 68 shaders: 235 selected (one record each), and 26 still whole tables.
- **All 26 whole tables are one heap:** base 0x204d400000, 1.5 MiB, 32,768 records of 8 dwords at a 48-byte stride, the same shape for the whole run. No sampler tables.
- **So it is NOT thrash.** All 26 share one memo key, about 0.3 Mi words, against the 4 Mi word, 16-entry budget.
- **Likely cause:** the vouch fails at every capture, because that heap is written or stamped every frame. Every capture then pays PM-028's stamp, two flushes, the read and compare, the fresh walk and the memo store, on top of main's work, which is its worst case. The bench said -8% to +12%; Village shows +58% of capture.
- **For this task,** the priority is the miss path's cost, not capacity.
  1. Make a capture whose vouch fails cost no more than main's (skip the extra flushes and stamp, and drop admission when a column keeps changing).
  2. Find out from the code what writes that per-lane heap every frame, and whether it really changes or only gets stamped. If it's only stamped, for example a write of equal data or a coarse stamp over the whole range, a finer check could make the reuse hit.
**diagP's counters (00:45 PT; they SUPERSEDE the 'vouch fails' guess above):** profiled boot of cut #3 + PM-028 (libs-tracy-scr, `APS5_PROFILE_DRAW=1`, warm cache, static menu), per 10 s:
- ~1,500 snapshots, ~6,500 tables, ~6,100 selected records;
- **~410 whole-table captures, `reused ~410 unwritten / 0 compared`: the reuse HITS every time, with no compares and no misses**;
- table snapshot time ~4,250 ms per 10 s (profiled);
- `[capture]` overall: 534,000 captures, 87 words read on average, walk 630 µs per capture, no GPU waits inside captures.

**So the HIT path is what costs:** ~164 whole-table captures per frame against 941 draws. ladC's +1,000 µs per draw comes to **~5.7 ms extra per reused capture on Windows under Village's load**, against 393-478 µs per hit in your Linux bench.

What the hit path does over the 1.5 MiB heap at every capture:
1. the pending-write query (`queryPendingWrite`);
2. `FlushGpuWrites`;
3. `CollectWrites` + `UnchangedSince` (GetWriteWatch on Windows);
4. handing the codes and distinct T#s over;
5. `supplyWords` copying ~1 MiB into the capture's pages.

Suspects: (1) and (2) scanning long lists under Village's load; Windows GetWriteWatch over the range; the 1 MiB copy and page churn per capture.

**The redesign's focus is now the hit path:**
- **Measure it under Windows-like conditions.** Ideally the Bug Finder runs PM-028's bench on GitHub's Windows runner; check results/ for its numbers. Add scoped timers inside the vouch (stamp, pending, flush, collect, supply) so one Village trace names the cost. Keep the timers in a drop commit if they shouldn't ship.
- **No per-capture copy:** the capture refers to the memo's immutable column (PM-032's shared column). This removes step 5 and probably most of 4.
- **Make steps 1-3 O(changed), not O(range):** keep a per-heap stamp that one check validates, instead of re-walking the range's pages and pending lists at every capture.
- **The hard requirement stays:** neither the hit path nor the miss path may cost more than main's capture, in Village.

**The Bug Finder's code-reading lead (unmeasured; verify it):** every reused capture runs `ShaderMemory::unchangedSince` over the whole 1.5 MiB heap:
1. `pendingWrite` → `Driver::queryPendingWrite` (WriteEvidence.cpp). It scales with recorded-but-unfinished writes: many in Village, none in the bench.
2. `GuestMemory::FlushGpuWrites`, which in a real run is the recorder's `FlushForAccess` (Recorder.cpp:1002). Per call it checks queued labels, takes **the global GpuMutex** unless the hook snapshot rules the range out, checks `PendingWriteOverlaps`, and scans the storage-texture registry once or twice (`StorageTexture::AccessKeptByCpu`, then `PublishShadowsOnly`/`FlushPending`). With many storage images and three busy workers on that mutex, this is likely the big one. **PM-028's bench has no recorder** ("without one (tests), pending storage results alone are flushed"), so it can't see it.
3. `CollectWrites`, memoized per collect epoch, else the global tracker lock plus a range walk (GetWriteWatch on Windows).
4. `UnchangedSince`: the tracker lock again plus 24 block checks.
5. `supplyWords` copying 1 MiB into the capture's 384 pages.

**Design hint:** cache "no pending write and nothing to flush over this range" per submission epoch, invalidated by a new recorded write or deferred result over the range. Then (1) and (2) run once per epoch, not per capture; the other write-watch consumers could use the same idea. PM-032's shared immutable column removes (5).

**Your bench must model Village's load:** an active recorder, a populated storage-texture registry, recorded-but-unfinished writes, and two or three threads contending, so (1) and (2) show up.

The Bug Finder's GitHub run 38701145952 measures PM-028's bench on Windows and Linux runners, reuse on and off. It isolates (3) and (4) only; its numbers will be added here.
