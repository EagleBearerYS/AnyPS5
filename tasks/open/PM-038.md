| frame p50 | 3,662 ms | **4,578 ms (+25%)** |

The pair is valid: equal draws per frame (35,741 vs 35,734 over 38 frames), the same image, no errors, a settled shader cache, and interval ranges that don't overlap.

## Likely cause (being confirmed on the PC; counters will be added below)
- PM-028's memo is per thread, 16 columns and 4 Mi 32-bit words.
- Village's whole tables (per-lane keys) are 65,536-record columns of 8 dwords, over 0.5 Mi words each. With more than about 7 distinct whole-table columns cycling per frame, the memo thrashes.
- Every capture then pays the pre-read stamp, the vouch, the flushes and a multi-MB copy into the memo, and is never reused. The worse `Driver.Draw` time fits cache pollution from those copies.
- **Second possibility:** the tables are written every frame, so the vouch always fails and the memo only adds work.

## Task
1. **Reproduce both scenarios in PM-028's microbenchmark** (the dropped bench commit f42558c9e8, locally): N distinct 65,536-record whole-table columns cycling (N = 4, 8, 16, 32, 64), and columns written before every capture. Give µs per capture against main and against PM-028.
2. **Redesign so the miss path costs no more than main's capture in every scenario,** within noise. Examples:
   - adaptive admission: memoize a column only after it has been seen unchanged twice, and skip the stamp and copy for one-off columns;
   - a capacity sized for Village's working set: a device-wide LRU shared across queue workers, with a byte budget (for example 64-128 MiB) and safe sharing;
   - no copy at all on admission: refer to the snapshot already captured, which is where PM-032's shared-column idea comes in.
   Keep PM-028's vouching rules exact.
3. **Fold in PM-032's O(1) unchanged path** if it fits the redesign.
4. **Tests:**
   - PM-028's and PM-032's tests;
   - new cases: thrash with many distinct columns (no slowdown, memo bounded) and always-written columns (no slowdown);
   - `^agc`.
