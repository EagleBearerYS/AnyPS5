**The short answer from main's code:** commits don't each submit or wait; submits are already batched. The ~300 µs is CPU work the committer does while it holds the GPU mutex, plus waits on that thread. Three things stand out:
- **The draw build runs on the committer.** `commitDraw` holds `GpuMutex` and runs `VulkanDevice::Draw`, so the worker's "[draw] driver phases" (~100 µs per packet) is only the worker's half of each draw.
- **Each queued dispatch builds and uploads its buffers on the committer under the lock.** The 52–63 ms uploads (~15 per 10 s, ≈0.9 s) and the 726 MiB copied in and back per 10 s come from here.
- **GPU waits on the committer:** locked fence waits, timeline waits, and the `WaitIdle` fallbacks of DMA stores, labels and fills.

Which one dominates is what the new line measures.

## Part 1: what each commit kind does on the committer

- **Lock waits count in the time:** every closure takes `GpuMutex` inside that timed span.

### Can the per-commit cost be batched?

- **Submits:** already batched (deadline, 64-item cap, EOP labels, end of submission). The split's "submit" field will show how small that part is.
- **Staging:** copy-in (`recordGpuCopies` from `UploadFinish` or `RecordStagingCopies`, G/GuestBufferMemory.cpp:5279-5387, 5995-6013) and copy-back (`RecordCopyBacks`, 5389-5470) are GPU `vkCmdCopyBuffer`s recorded into the open batch, on every use.
  - The shadow is per use, not resident (comment at 4570). So copy-in repeats on every reuse and copy-back after every use.
  - Their CPU cost is the recording plus new shadows. The GPU time reaches the committer only through its waits.
  - Batching needs resident shadows (Part 3, fix 1).
- **The draw build:** per draw by nature. It could move off the committer (fix 2).
- **Proofs:** `BreakProofs` is global (one atomic bump), and voids every epoch proof (G/ShaderResources.cpp:3421-3447, G/Texture.cpp:2094). Scoping it to a commit's writes would let draws after a label or seal keep their proof. But `[rescache] revalidate` is only 241 ms per 10 s here, so the ceiling is small.

### The three questions

**a) Why the dispatch device call looks up `vkGetDeviceProcAddr` ~56 times per dispatch.**
- **What the counter counts:** the "[vk]" line (E/VulkanDevice.cpp:5557) is the change in a thread-local count (GI/Context.hpp:147-150) over one dispatch. Every `Context::Function` (Context.hpp:285-291) and `DeviceFunction` (VulkanDevice.cpp:590-598) call adds one.
- **The cache exists:** `DeviceFunctions` (Context.hpp:100-143), filled once per device and read through `Context::Resolved` (295-298). The Recorder has its own table (Recorder.cpp:1149-1165). The hot `recordDispatch` path already uses them.
- **Sites that still look up every time:**
  - pooled buffers with a size mismatch or a pool miss: `vkDestroyBuffer`/`vkCreateBuffer`/`vkGetBufferMemoryRequirements`/`vkBindBufferMemory`/`vkGetBufferDeviceAddressKHR`, 4–5 per buffer (G/Resources.cpp:24-30, 51-60, 74-101; BufferAddress.cpp:13);
  - 38 direct `vkCmdPipelineBarrier` lookups in G/Texture.cpp, though the table has it;
  - `vkAllocateDescriptorSets` (ShaderResources.cpp:2201, 2269);
  - host-import creation;
  - under `APS5_PROFILE_DRAW`: `vkGetFenceStatus`, `vkGetQueryPoolResults`, `vkCmdWriteTimestamp` (Recorder.cpp:4118, 2625, 2048/2055);
  - pipeline creation on a cache miss.
  - I can't split the exact 56 without a run, and draws hit the same sites uncounted.
- **The fix:**
  - **Cost:** about 25 more `DeviceFunctions` entries (one lookup each per device), with these sites switched to `Resolved`.
  - **Saving** (*inferred*, at 0.2–1 µs per loader lookup): ~10–55 µs per dispatch, so ≤0.1 s per 10 s at 1,555 dispatches. Cheap, but not the bottleneck.

**b) What notes 285 M "in-place reads" that nothing queries.**
- **Who notes them:** `NotePendingRead`, `NotePendingReads` and `NotePendingReadList` (G/Recorder.cpp:3195-3233), on by default via `APS5_COPY_READ_TRACKING` (586-594).
- **Which thread:** whichever thread records, under `GpuMutex` (`ensureOpen` asserts it, 1574). For queue 0 that is the committer: `MarkGpuWrites`, GPU copies (GuestBufferMemory.cpp:5360), draw inputs (G/Draw.cpp:1243), indirect arguments, storage uploads.
- **Granularity:** one entry per in-place range. An address-based build's heap list (~1,200 regions) is counted by length but stored once as a shared pointer, so 285 M overstates the work.
- **Cost per note:** a 24-byte `push_back`, one relaxed atomic add, and a linear duplicate scan of `readLists`. No mutex of its own.
- **Why nothing queries them:** the only reader is `PendingReadOverlaps` (3270-3292), reached only from `CopyBuffer`'s CPU path (VulkanDevice.cpp:2910). DMA copies pass `cpuMax` 0, so it is never reached.
- *Inferred:* well under 1% of the committer. Turning it off while nothing can query it is safe and cheap, but small.

**c) Which path does the ~70 MiB/s staging copy-in and copy-back, and does it run on the committer.**
- **The path:** queued dispatches' compute builds; only compute builds stage (`AllowDeviceStaging`, ShaderResources.cpp:1623).
- **Which regions:** `stagingEligible` (GuestBufferMemory.cpp:4721-4727) takes a written, non-mirrored, non-sparse region that is either atomic and ≤1 MiB, or 16 KiB to 2 MiB (8 MiB on a discrete GPU; swept regions 16 or 256 MiB). The average here is ≈640 KiB (726 MB over 1,132 regions).
- **The copies:** GPU copies recorded into the Recorder's open command buffer, by the committer, under the lock. Copy-in comes from `UploadFinish` (5096-5113) or `RecordStagingCopies` on reuse; copy-back from `RecordCopyBacks`. Not CPU memcpys, and not the settle queue (that only creates imports).
- **On the committer:** the recording, plus `UploadPrepare`/`UploadFinish`'s guest reads for unstaged regions. The `[resources] guest memory upload took 52–63 ms` lines are those uploads in stage B `Complete()` under the lock.
- **Possible correctness gap, unverified:** the epoch fast path in `Revalidate` (ShaderResources.cpp:3426-3436) skips `revalidateFull`, and so skips `RecordStagingCopies`, leaving `copiedBack` set. On the committer `BreakProofs` after each dispatch prevents it. Back-to-back dispatches run inline on the worker might not.
