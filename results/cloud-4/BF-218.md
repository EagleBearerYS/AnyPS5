## What was checked, and how

**Design reference:** #5640 on main (merge d01070d6c3; its commits' texts in `git log d01070d6c3^1..d01070d6c3^2`). Every site:
- covers whole instructions and is at least 5 bytes;
- is refused when a branch lands inside it or it overlaps the prologue, an exit, another site or an existing stub;
- copies no branch, `ret` or RIP-relative instruction;
- moves each RSP operand by the shift, in place or re-encoded in a stub.

Line numbers below are in `core/relinker/codegen/src/x86/RedZoneFramer.cpp` at 07798774b6.

### 1. Short exits (`:356-374`, `:467-473`)

**Borrowing rule:** the exit takes the instructions before its first pop or freeing instruction (`q`), walking back while the site is under 5 bytes and `First > p`, so never into the prologue. It stops at:
- anything not `Sequential`, which covers `jmp`, `jcc`, `loop`/`jrcxz`, `ret`, `ud2` and `ud1`;
- RIP-relative instructions;
- the freeing instruction;
- any push or pop (`pushesOrPops`).

**Position independence:** I checked the decoder (`X64InstructionDecoder.cpp:402-404`, `:424`, `:441-547`).
- VEX, EVEX and XOP are `Sequential`, and their RIP-relative operands set `HasRipRelativeDisp`.
- `loop`, `jrcxz` and every `jcc` are branches.
- So every borrowed instruction is position-independent or refused.
- The one exception is `xbegin` (`C7 F8 rel32`), which decodes as sequential. RTM does not exist on Zen 2, so a PS5 binary has none. Main's operand stubs have the same exposure.

**RSP operands:** each borrowed RSP operand is re-encoded with `RspDisp + shift` (`:365`), and it runs before `raiseRsp`, so RSP is still lowered by exactly `shift`. `carried()` (`:402-408`) removes these instructions from the in-place moves and the operand-stub list, so no displacement patch lands inside the jump.

**Flags and registers:** the stub is `Lead`, then `lea rsp,[rsp+shift]`, then the original tail. Original order is lead, tail. `lea` writes only RSP and leaves the flags alone, and the tail's `add rsp,n` sees the same RSP value as before. So flags and every other register leave the stub as the original sequence leaves them.

**Branch targets:** `targetInside(exit.Begin, exit.End)` (`:373`) refuses any target after `Begin`, including the original tail's first instruction. A target at `Begin` lands on the jump to the stub and runs lead plus tail, as the original would.

**Probes:**
- P1 and P1b: a leaf without pushes with two `ret`s, each with a 5-byte load before it, the second reached by `jcc` to its lead. Both paths frame, and both return under the clobber.
- P2: a lead carrying `xor rax,[rsp]` (mod 00), which can't be moved in place. It is re-encoded with a disp8 and returns the right value.
- P3: a lead carrying VEX `andn rax,rcx,[rsp-8]` frames and is correct.

### 2. Short prologues (`:380-400`, `:462-466`)

**Borrowing rule:** the entry stub runs the original prologue, then `lea rsp,[rsp-shift]`, then the borrowed instructions with their RSP operands moved by the shift. They run after lowering, so the shift is right.
- The same stop conditions as the exits apply.
- Widening also stops before reaching an exit (`:387`).
- `:400` is a backstop for an exit that begins before `entryEnd`, which the earlier break already prevents.

**Jumps into the middle of the prologue:**
- `targetInside(start, entryEnd)` (`:397`) refuses any target inside the original or the widened prologue.
- `:398` also refuses a target at `start` when the prologue was widened. That matters: in a leaf without pushes, a loop back to its first instruction would run `lea rsp,[rsp-shift]` a second time.

**Probes:**
- P4: a 4-byte `sub rsp,8` prologue widened over a store frames and is correct. No existing test has an allocation with a widened prologue.
- P7: a loop to the instruction after a 1-byte push is refused with "branch into the instructions after a short prologue".

### 3. Leaves without pushes or an allocation

**Entry:** RSP is lowered on entry (`p = 0`, entry body = `lea` plus the borrowed instructions) and raised before every `ret`.

**Exits:** the main loop builds an exit at every `ret` (`:350-374`), and any exit that can't be built refuses the leaf. So no `ret` is left unraised.
- `ret imm16` and `rep ret` are refused ("ret with an immediate", `:351`).
- A tail call by `jmp` or `jcc` outside the FDE is refused ("branch leaves the function", `:346-348`), and so is an indirect jump.

**RSP accesses:** every RSP-relative access from `k = 0` is moved, stubbed or carried. Any other use of RSP as a register is refused (`:337`).

**Push and pop rule (`:333`):** any push or pop in these leaves refuses them.
- This is required: a fragment that pops what its caller pushed would read the wrong slot with RSP lowered.
- `pushesOrPops` (`:50-58`) covers `50`-`5F` (including `push rsp`, which `pushedRegister` excludes), `68`, `6A`, `9C`, `9D`, `8F /0`, `FF /6` and `0F A0`/`A1`/`A8`/`A9`, and skips VEX and EVEX. A VEX opcode in `50`-`5F`, such as `vxorps`, is not misread as a push.
- `enter` and `leave` have their own reason, and `call` makes the function not a leaf.

**Probes:**
- P8: a `ret` straight after a `jcc` is refused ("exit shorter than a jump").
- P10: `push 1` in such a leaf is refused.

### 4. The rbp-only case (07798774b6)

**What changed:** `!rbpPrologue` now exempts `push rbp; mov rbp,rsp` leaves from the rule above. The old code counted their `pop rbp` as a pop without a push.

**Frame pointer:**
- Such a leaf takes the 128-byte shift (`wholeRedZone = rbpPrologue`) and refuses every RSP use (`:335`). The red zone is reached only through rbp, which the prologue set before lowering.
- Its pops and pushes keep #5640's handling: the exit's pops must be exactly `[rbp, pushes...]`, and pushes and pops in the body only move RSP within the lowered frame.
- P6, with a body `push rbx`/`pop rbx` and a red-zone slot at `[rbp-16]`, frames and returns the unframed result under the clobber.

**Only saved, without `mov rbp,rsp`:** rbp is an ordinary entry in `pushed`, so the rule never applied.
- P5 (`push rbp`, rbp used as a scratch register, `pop rbp; ret`) widens both sites and is correct.

**What can't be framed is covered:**
- Branches, `ret`, `ud2`, RIP-relative instructions, pushes and pops, and the freeing instruction all stop widening, and the site is then refused if still short.
- EVEX RSP operands, indirect jumps, other RSP arithmetic, overlapping sites, `ret imm16`, `enter`/`leave` and branches out of the FDE keep #5640's reasons.

**Missed coverage, not a defect:** `:398` refuses a widened leaf whose first instruction is the target of any `jmp` in the segment, including a tail jump from another function.
- Such a jump arrives with the caller's RSP, so the entry stub would be right for it.
- P11, a leaf without pushes reached by a tail `jmp`, is refused.
- P12, the same with a 5-byte push prologue that is not widened, frames.
- Only branches from inside the function need refusing. This costs coverage, not correctness.
