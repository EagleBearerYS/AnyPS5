# BF-055: the Village harness's growth is live builds kept by un-retired recorder batches, not dead chain-pool sets (cloud-4)

**Arm A:** fork `exp/bf046-main` b7d6d98610 (upstream main 16c607a937, BF-042's harness commits and `VillageDrawCost.cpp`).
- **Instrumentation:** fork `exp/descriptor-set-release-measure` **1a90ccb64**, 3 commits on b7d6d98610.
- **Builds measured:**
  - a41ab28ad's tree for the census, the three 60-frame runs and the short synced run;
  - 1a90ccb64 for the kept-bytes run and the 100,000-draw run.
- **Fix branch:** no `exp/descriptor-set-release`, because step 5 applies. Nothing else was pushed.

**Machine:** cloud-4: Xeon @ 2.10 GHz, 4 vCPUs, 15 GiB, Ubuntu 24.04, gcc 13.3.0, wild 0.10.0, lavapipe (Mesa 25.2.8, LLVM 20.1.2).
- **Settings:** BF-046's: as `runner`, `ANYPS5_REQUIRE_VULKAN=1 GALLIUM_OVERRIDE_CPU_CAPS=avx ANYPS5_NO_SHADER_CACHE=1 LP_NUM_THREADS=2`, with `vm.max_map_count` raised to 1,048,576.
- **Runs:** `--cost 52` is 8 warm-up and 52 measured frames: 60 frames, 51,000 draws, plus the check's draws. One run at a time on an idle machine. Only the harness and its synthetic shaders were used.

RESULT:
- **Cause: live builds, held by the recorder's in-flight batches.**
  - **The GPU never catches up.** lavapipe needs about 10 s of GPU per frame of this shape (median 10,073 ms with the GPU waited for), while the CPU produces a frame in about 0.25 s.
  - **Nothing makes the CPU wait for the GPU:**
    - the harness waits in `AgcDriverWaitIdle`, which is `Driver::WaitIdle`: `completed >= accepted` on the worker, not the GPU;
    - it has no flips;
    - `Recorder::Reap` retires only signaled batches;
    - the recorder's own bound, `BoundKeptBytes` (2 × 512 MiB), counts a kept draw's `CopiedBytes()`. That is about 840 bytes per address-based build, 11.8 MB at 15,000 draws, against about 850 MB of real growth.
  - **What piles up:**
    - after 51,000 draws, 811 submitted batches are in flight and the GPU has completed up to serial 36 of 847;
    - those batches keep 49,275 of the 49,295 live `ShaderResources`;
    - each build holds 2 chain-pool descriptor sets, and lavapipe gives each set one 4 KiB shared memfd page;
    - so maps reach 105,012 and RSS 3.1 GB.
- **Neither switch stops it.** `APS5_NO_POOL_CACHE=1` (no chain pools at all) and `APS5_NO_DRAW_RESOURCE_CACHE=1` (the resource cache was already empty) grow exactly as the baseline does.
- **Back-pressure stops it.** With `VILLAGE_GPU_SYNC=1`, which waits for the GPU after each frame as a flip would, maps stay between 2,291 and 3,929 over 100,000 draws (2,499 at the start, 3,301 at the end). RSS stays between 302 and 447 MB, the in-flight batches stay at 0–11, and exit takes 0.01 s.
- **Dead chain-pool sets: real, but not the cause here.**
  - The one chain pool never resets while any set in it is live, so a retired build's sets stay allocated in the driver: 1,700, then 3,400 by the end of the 60-frame run.
  - With the GPU synced there are 0: the pool empties between frames, because nothing long-lived holds a set (resource cache 0, recycled 0).
  - On lavapipe the pool also never fills: one pool with `maxSets` 1,024 held 101,990 sets, so lavapipe does not enforce `maxSets`. On a real driver the same pinning would show as new pools, which fits the PC's 26 → 38 pools in 12 minutes but does not prove it.
- **Process exit (about 580 s on all three runs) is the GPU draining that backlog.** Both `llvmpipe` threads stay at 95–100% for the whole exit, and the mappings stay at 105,026 until the end. 60 frames × about 10 s is about 600 s.
- **Per step 5, no fix:** `DescriptorCache` is not the holder here. The fix this harness needs is in the recorder; it is proposed under "What to fix" but not built.
- **Found on the way:** BF-046's "GPU per draw" on lavapipe is the two rasterizer threads saturated for the frame window, about 2 × wall. The real GPU cost of this shape is about 23,300 µs of rasterizer CPU per draw (§5).

## 3. The mappings that grow

From `/proc/<pid>/maps` and `smaps`, sampled every 15 s from outside:

| | at start | after 51,000 draws |
|---|---|---|
| all mappings | 377 | 105,026 |
| file-backed | 254 | 104,839 |
| anonymous | 117 | 181 |
| `/memfd:allocation fd (deleted)`, `rw-s`, 4 KiB | n/a | **103,807** |
| `/memfd:allocation fd (deleted)`, `rw-s`, 2 MiB | n/a | 794 |
| anonymous RSS | n/a | 1,599 MB |

- **What grows:** shared, file-backed, one-page memfd mappings. "allocation fd" is the name llvmpipe gives its memfd-backed memory allocations. They aren't anonymous, and they aren't our buffers: our guest and staging buffers are among the anonymous and the 2 MiB mappings, which barely move.
- **One mapping per descriptor set the driver holds:**
  - baseline: 105,012 maps − 101,990 allocated chain sets = 3,022;
  - `APS5_NO_POOL_CACHE`: 101,611 maps − 98,590 sets (2 per live build) = 3,021;
  - synced run: maps − allocated chain sets stays at 2,206–2,223 whether the pool holds 90 or 1,490 sets.

  So each lavapipe descriptor set has one 4 KiB memfd page as its storage. This is lavapipe's descriptor-set storage, and it lives exactly as long as our sets do.

## 4. The cause, with back-pressure as the control

`VILLAGE_GPU_SYNC=1` (a41ab28ad, and with timing 1a90ccb64) flushes the target's GPU writes after every frame, which is what a flip's pacing would do. It runs 100,000 draws (`--cost 110`, 118 frames):

| 20,000 | 3,133 | 389 | 445 | 1 | 890 | 0 | 7 | 441 | 353 / 360 | 55 |

| 100,000 | 3,301 | 445 | 545 | 1 | 1,090 | 0 | 8 | 507 | 1763 / 1771 | 83 |

- **Flat over 100,000 draws.**
  - Maps over all 100 samples: 2,291–3,929, starting at 2,499 and ending at 3,301. They rise within a frame and fall when its batches retire.
  - RSS over all samples: 302–447 MB. It stepped from about 390 to about 445 MB once, near 80,000 draws, and then stayed there.
  - Dead chain sets: 0 throughout.
- **The GPU's cost, which the backlog had hidden:** the GPU finished each frame a median **10,073 ms** after its submission returned (min 9,731, max 11,088). That's about 11.9 ms of wall time per draw on 2 rasterizer threads, against 0.14–0.25 s of CPU per frame.
- **The rest of the run:**
  - The CPU-side frame was a median 139 ms (164 µs per draw), and driver CPU 569 µs per draw. The worker's 413 µs includes its wait inside the flush.
  - The whole run took 1,213 s, and exit took **0.01 s**.

**Why the CPU runs away without back-pressure:**
- `Driver::WaitIdle` (Lifecycle.cpp:168) returns once the worker has processed the submission (`completed >= accepted`).
- `Recorder::Reap` (Recorder.cpp:3724 at arm A) stops at the first unsignaled fence.
- `Recorder::BoundKeptBytes` (Recorder.cpp:2519) waits only once the in-flight kept bytes pass 2 × `KeptBytesBudget` (512 MiB). But `keepRecordedDraw` (Draw.cpp:1511-1522 at arm A) adds only `resources->CopiedBytes()`.
- The kept-bytes run (`--cost 10`, 1a90ccb64) shows how little that is:

  | draws | in-flight batches | kept builds | in-flight kept bytes | RSS |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 3,000 | 35 | 2,141 | 1,798,440 | |
  | 9,000 | 133 | 8,091 | 6,796,440 | |
  | 15,000 | 232 | 14,105 | 11,848,200 | 1,147 MB |

  That is about 840 bytes counted per kept build, against about 60 KB of real growth per build. The bound would first trigger at about 1.27 million in-flight builds.
- **A game behaves differently:** it flips, and flip pacing (`VulkanDevice::FlipInFlight`, 1 by default) makes the presenter wait for the GPU. That bounds this backlog. The harness never flips, so on a GPU slower than the CPU it has no bound at all.

**Is it lavapipe's own behaviour? Partly.**
- lavapipe's slowness creates the backlog: a 2,000-entry T# table, sampled 1–4 times per pixel, at 1080p on 2 threads.
- Its one-page memfd per descriptor set turns the backlog into mappings and the `vm.max_map_count` crash.
- But the sets do go back to the driver once their builds die (§4). It is the driver that keeps the builds without a bound its accounting can see.

## 5. Found on the way: BF-046's GPU column on lavapipe is saturation

- **BF-046 §3's "GPU (rasterizer CPU) per draw" is about twice its wall per draw:**
  - arm A: 686.5 against 2 × 347.2 = 694.4;
  - arm B: 341.9 against 2 × 174.5 = 349.0.
- **Why:** the two `llvmpipe` threads were busy for the whole frame window on a backlog that never drained, so the column measured 2 threads × the window, not the frame's GPU work.
- **Measured with the GPU waited for:**
  - this shape costs about **23,300 µs of rasterizer CPU per draw** (median of 110 frames, synced run; 22,882 in the 3-frame synced run), about 34 times BF-046's 687;
  - our unsynced runs show 563–594, also 2 × the window.
- **What it changes:** K4's GPU half ("arm B costs half of arm A") was measured the same way and should be re-measured with `VILLAGE_GPU_SYNC=1` before it is relied on.

## 6. Process exit

**The ~580 s exit is the backlog draining.**
- After the 60-frame runs, exit took 587 s (baseline), 577 s (`APS5_NO_POOL_CACHE`) and 580 s (`APS5_NO_DRAW_RESOURCE_CACHE`) after the last output.
- A per-thread CPU sample every 5 s through the baseline's exit shows only `llvmpipe-0` and `llvmpipe-1` at 95–100%, for the whole 587 s. Every driver thread was idle, and the mappings stayed at 105,026 until the process ended.
- 60 frames × about 10 s of GPU each is about 600 s.
- After an 18-frame run, exit took 170 s (18 × about 10 s).
- With `VILLAGE_GPU_SYNC=1`, exit took 0.01 s after 118 frames.
- **No profile needed:** the static destruction itself is cheap once the GPU is done.

## What to fix (not done: step 5)

1. **The harness's growth needs a recorder bound that sees what a kept build costs.** For example:
   - `keepRecordedDraw` adds a kept build's descriptor sets and its non-copied buffers to `KeepBytes`;
   - or `BoundKeptBytes` also caps the number of in-flight batches or kept builds, so a GPU this far behind makes the committer wait on the oldest batch.

   Either is a recorder change on current main, whose recorder is unchanged since arm A. The checks would be the synced figures above without the synced switch: maps flat over 100,000 draws, and CPU per draw at the cost of the waits.
2. **The dead-set pinning in chain pools is real but unmeasured where it would matter.** The census's pool histogram, run once on the PC at Village's menu with `APS5_BF055_CENSUS=1` on `exp/descriptor-set-release-measure`, would show whether its 26 → 38 pools are pinned by a few live sets each.
   - If so, BF-055's step 4 applies: `VK_DESCRIPTOR_POOL_CREATE_FREE_DESCRIPTOR_SET_BIT` chain pools, `vkFreeDescriptorSets` in `Free`, and reuse of a pool with room.
   - It must update `descriptorPoolTests` in `tests/Graphics.cpp`, which asserts today that chain pools have no FREE bit ("descriptor pools use the individually freed allocation path") and that an exhausted pool is not retried before its reset.
   - lavapipe can't show it as pools, since it ignores `maxSets`. It can only show it as memory, and only if something long-lived holds a set.
