- 11 × `the queue-idle store ran before the fixture could take the GPU mutex` (:130);

So GitHub's failure reproduces here.

### What `AgcDriverWaitIdle_nid_postfix` does after the drain

I checked this by timing, not by assumption, using two local diagnostic builds. Neither is committed or pushed, and the driver library was rebuilt byte-identical afterwards (`cmp` against the saved `.prx`).

- **`Driver::WaitIdle`** (`Execution/src/Driver/Lifecycle.cpp:237-250`):
  - waits for `completed >= accepted` (:239-247);
  - then takes the GPU mutex (:248);
  - then calls `VulkanDevice::WaitIdle` (:249).
- **`VulkanDevice::WaitIdle`** (`Execution/src/VulkanDevice.cpp:2461-2476`), timed per step in 52 calls under (d):

  | Step | Time |
  |---|---|
  | `StorageTexture::StoreDrawnLinear("wait idle")` (:2463) | stored **0** targets in 0.0 ms, every time |
  | `RetirePresents` | 0.0 ms |
  | `recorder->Sync()` (:2465-2470) | **12.4 – 168.2 ms** (median 47.5). The recorder was never idle: Sync submits the packet's still-open batch and waits for lavapipe to run the two dispatches |
  | `vkDeviceWaitIdle` (:2473) | at most 4.6 ms |

  So the time is not in storing drawn linear targets. It is `Recorder::Sync` running the batch that the queue left open, which on lavapipe includes llvmpipe compiling the compute shader.

**When the quiet period starts.** It starts at the drain, before `WaitIdle` takes the mutex. The sequence:
1. The queue marks the submission completed right after executing it (`QueueWorker.cpp:282`, :293).
2. It goes back to its loop. `completionsPending()` (`DeferredLabels.cpp:79`) counts only labels and write-back completions, so after plain dispatches it is false.
3. `idleResultsPending()` therefore holds, and the 50 ms `IdleResultsQuietPeriod` (`QueueWorker.cpp:20`, :203-216) starts at once, at the same point `WaitIdle`'s drain returns.
4. `WaitIdle` then holds the GPU mutex for its Sync. When that lasts past 50 ms, the queue reaches `StoreIdleResults` (`QueueWorker.cpp:219` → `VulkanDevice.cpp:2487-2493`) and blocks on the GPU mutex.
5. When `WaitIdle` releases it, the queue and the fixture both want the mutex.

**The diagnostic timings bear this out.** In 30 runs under (d), "between images" sub-test:
- `WaitIdle` returned 22.6–171.7 ms after the queue finished recording.
- **The images stayed pending in 16 runs.** In every one of them the fixture took the lock within 0.1 ms of `WaitIdle` returning, even when `WaitIdle` took 171.7 ms.
- **The idle store won in 14 runs.** In every one of them `WaitIdle` had returned at least 52.5 ms after recording, and the fixture got the lock 10.9–132.6 ms after that. It had been waiting while the queue's idle store held the mutex and stored both images.

The instrumented build gave the same split: 22 kept and 7 won, each won run at ≥ 51.8 ms.

**Is it lavapipe-specific?** Partly. The race itself is not: on any device, a `WaitIdle` that holds the mutex past 50 ms gives the queue's idle store a turn. But the long `Sync` is mostly llvmpipe compiling the shader when the batch first runs, with no shader cache, slowed further by CPU load. On a real GPU the batch would finish in well under 50 ms. Thread scheduling matters too (§2).

## 2. The fix: hold the queue until the fixture holds the GPU mutex

**First attempt: polling alone, not kept.** I first did what the task suggests: poll `AgcDriverSerialProgress_nid_postfix` every 200 µs until the packet's serial completed, then take the lock.
- **Why that counter works as a "recorded" signal:**
  - `Submit` assigns `serial = accepted + 1` and increments `accepted` under the driver mutex before it returns (`Submission.cpp:505`, :520-525).
  - `completed` reaches that serial only after `execute()` has recorded both dispatches (`QueueWorker.cpp:293`).
  - The deferred-completion path is used only with `APS5_DRAW_PIPELINE` (`Draw.cpp:3938`), which nothing sets here.
- **It is not deterministic.** Across 560 runs under (d), in five batches (three of them diagnostic builds of the same design), it failed twice with the same message and crashed five times. In the failing run that logged its timings, the fixture's 200 µs sleep returned after **132.1 ms**, and taking the lock cost another 47.6 ms. The quiet period counts wall time from the queue's completion, so any approach where the fixture must be scheduled within 50 ms can lose on a loaded runner. No polling interval fixes that.
- **Pending state can't serve either:** `StorageTexture::AnyPendingOverlaps` (`Graphics/src/Texture.cpp:4936`) answers per texture, so the 8 MiB image is pending over its far row as soon as the first dispatch is recorded.

**The kept fix makes the order deterministic, test-only, with the same observe-then-lock shape:**
1. **A wait at the end of the packet.** After the two dispatches, the packet ends with a `WAIT_REG_MEM` on a label in the test: `betweenImagesHold` / `withinAnImageHold` at :29-30, and `QueueHold::Append` at :120. The packet words match what `tests/Flip.cpp:242` already uses.
2. **The observable.** `SubmitAndWaitHeld` (:137-145) polls `AgcDriverAwaitedWaits_nid_postfix` every 200 µs through `QueueHold::Reached` (:125-129) until the queue waits on that label.
   - The queue publishes the awaited address before its wait loop: `noteWaitBlocked`, `WaitMemory.cpp:331` → `Submission.cpp:626-629`. It is read back by `Driver::AwaitedWaits` (`Lifecycle.cpp:263-278`), exported at `EntryPoints.cpp:147`.
   - The queue reaches that wait only after recording both dispatches.
3. **Lock, then release.** The fixture then takes the GPU mutex, and only then stores the label (`hold.Release()`, :163 and :195).
4. **Why the idle store can't run first.** The idle store runs only in queue 0's own loop (`QueueWorker.cpp:199-231`, the only caller of `StoreIdleResults`). That loop is inside `execute()` until the wait is satisfied, and the wait loop (`WaitMemory.cpp:330-400`) stores no pending results; its `FlushGpuWrites` covers only the label's 4 bytes. So the queue cannot finish the packet, and its 50 ms quiet period cannot even start, before the fixture holds the mutex. No timing margin is involved.
5. **The failure path.** `~QueueHold` (:116) stores the label too, so a throwing check never leaves the queue blocked. That is also why the labels are static: they must outlive the queue's last read.
6. **Unchanged:** the test's two sub-tests, every check and every message, including :164's "queue-idle store ran before …", which is now unreachable rather than racy. `ANYPS5_RELINKER_ONLY` and the driver are untouched.

**Seam:** none needed. With a seam (a quiet-period setter, or a hold on the idle store) this could be simpler, but the hold label does it from the test alone.
