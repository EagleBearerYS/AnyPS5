**Terminate:**
- **From inside the callback** (`InEventCallback`, `:147`: `scePthreadEqual(scePthreadSelf(), delivering)`): returns `SAVE_DATA_ERROR_BUSY` before anything else, as the source does, and never joins.
- **Otherwise, the existing checks:** `NOT_INITIALIZED`; `BUSY` for the last one with a mounted slot.
- **The last one:** `DetachEventDelivery` (`:130`) clears the registration and the queue, as the source's terminate unregisters, and flags the worker to stop. Then, **outside `g_slots_mutex`**, `JoinEventDelivery` (`:141`) wakes and joins the thread.

**Why it can't deadlock:**
- **Terminate holds no SaveData lock while it joins.** A callback still running, say one that mounts, finishes and the thread exits.
- **A terminate from inside the callback returns** without touching the thread.
- **Each thread has its own stop flag** (`EventWorker`). A re-initialize while an old thread is still leaving starts a new one that the old one can't confuse.

**Why no TLS:**
- **The source:** it uses a thread-local flag.
- **Here:** I compare the guest thread handle (`delivering`) instead, as libSceUsbStorage does.
- **The reason:** on Windows, libc's `HostThreadLocal` uses fiber-local storage, so a plain `thread_local` on guest threads isn't the established pattern.

**Debt:**
- **The pending list:** both names are out of it.
- **The new "Functional" entry** (914 characters) says:
  - the behaviour is from shadPS4 #5226, not measured on a console, and PS5's signatures are assumed to be PS4's;
  - unknown on the console: the delivering thread, its priority and affinity, when it runs relative to the call, whether unregistering drops undelivered completions (here it does), and what a terminate from the callback does (here `BUSY`, as in shadPS4);
  - what this implementation does.
