## The cause, with file:line

The scene's color is the guest's own PERSP_SAMPLE barycentrics. The guest pixel shader `SampleColorCode` exports `(v0, v1, sample id, sample id)`:
- `v0`/`v1` are `PERSP_SAMPLE` I/J;
- the export is `SPI_SHADER_32_ABGR` (color format 9), into an `R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT` target.

So channel 0 is I, channel 1 is J, rounded to f16 by the target.

| Step | Where | Can it give one f16 step? |
|---|---|---|
| Sample swap | `Graphics/shaders/SampleOrder.comp:22-41`: `imageLoad`/`imageStore` of `uvec4` through a `uimage2DMS` alias (`SampleOrderStorageFormat`, `Graphics/src/SampleOrder.cpp:586`) | **No.** It moves the f16 bits unchanged. |
| Host locations | `HostSampleLocationsFor`, `Graphics/src/SampleOrder.cpp:620`: pixel (0,0)'s set stands in for every pixel; odd rows are swapped | **No.** Every guest sample is drawn at its own guest location. Depth, drawn by fixed-function hardware at those locations, matches in 3005/3005 samples with #9503. |
| Sample index | `GuestSampleIndex`, `SpirvBackend/src/SpirvModuleEmitter.cpp:87-91` | **No.** Channels 2/3 hold the sample index and match (`CheckScene`). |
| **Barycentric at the sample** | `Translation/src/InstructionTranslator.cpp:388-398`: with interpolation functions and fragment-shader barycentrics and off-centre locations, PERSP_SAMPLE becomes `BaryCoordSmoothSample`, which `SpirvModuleEmitter.cpp:160` evaluates as **`GLSLstd450InterpolateAtOffset`** at the guest's offset | **Yes, this one.** The offset comes from `EmitSampleOffset`, `SpirvModuleEmitter.cpp:125-143`. In the exact path the guest's locations vary by pixel, so the table has 32 entries indexed by guest sample index + quad parity (`:137`, via `EmitQuadPixel`). Each composite render has uniform locations: an 8-entry table indexed by sample index only. The same numeric offset therefore reaches the driver through two different shaders. |
| Render-target conversion | f32 → f16 by the colour target | **Not a source by itself:** the same hardware converts both sides. But it is why a sub-f16 f32 difference shows up as exactly one f16 step. |
| Blend/resolve | none on either side (blend off, no resolve) | No. |

**What fits second-channel-only, one step, edge-only:** the `InterpolateAtOffset` evaluation of the barycentric (`SpirvModuleEmitter.cpp:160`, offset from `:125-143`).
- **Even rows rule out the swap and the locations:** two of the four printed samples, (33,2) s1 and (34,2) s0, are on even rows. There the exact path neither swaps samples nor moves locations; host locations and sample index equal the composite's part-1 render. Only the pixel shader around `InterpolateAtOffset` differs.
- **Every printed sample is at an edge:** all four are at the apex of the shaded triangle (vertex at pixel (34, 1)). There every 2×2 quad is partly outside the triangle, and the shader's quad neighbours are helper lanes.
- **Not explained:** I can't account for why the driver's result moves in J and never in I from the code alone, and I can't reproduce it without an RDNA 4 GPU.

## Case (b): precision the spec leaves to the implementation

**The guest gets the right samples.** Each sample is at its own guest location, in the guest's order, with the right sample index and bit-exact depth. What differs is the last bits of a barycentric the driver computes in two differently shaped shaders.

**The spec text** (Vulkan-Docs at commit `70304594e73a065c7da6485165a59ff3d928e719`, `appendices/spirvenv.adoc`):
- **L4188-4189:** "GLSL.std.450 extended instructions not listed above and not defined in terms of the above have implementation-defined precision." `InterpolateAtOffset` is not in the precision table (its only mentions, L2313-2339, are offset range limits).
- **L3818-3828:** "Implementations may rearrange floating-point operations using any of the mathematical properties governing the expressions in precise arithmetic … and may involve a different number of rounding steps than would occur if the operations were not rearranged. This rearrangement can be prevented for particular operations by using the NoContraction decoration."

**Neither side is the exact value.** For the four printed samples I computed the exact barycentrics with rational arithmetic (vertices at integer pixels, w = 1, guest locations ±4/16). On RDNA 4 both renders are up to 1.6 f16 steps from the exact J. The exact path is the nearer one in every case:

| sample | exact J | exact path (RX 9070 GRE) | composite reference | exact path off by | reference off by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| s1 of (33, 1) | 0.982010 | 0.981934 (0x3f7b6000) | 0.982422 | −0.16 step | +0.84 step |
| s0 of (34, 1) | 0.980769 | 0.980469 | 0.979980 | −0.62 | −1.62 |
| s1 of (33, 2) | 0.946030 | 0.945801 | 0.945313 | −0.47 | −1.47 |
| s0 of (34, 2) | 0.942308 | 0.941895 | 0.942871 | −0.85 | +1.15 |

- At s1 of (33, 1), the exact path's 0x3f7b6000 is the correctly rounded f16 of the exact barycentric; the reference's is not.
- I matches between the two renders, but on RDNA 4 it is about one f16 step under its correctly rounded value too: 0x3bb1c000 against 0x3bb1e000.

So the reference composite is not "truth" either. The difference is the device's interpolation precision, not the exact path.

## The change (71738a8394)

**Edge samples.** An *edge sample* is one whose 2×2 quad, taken at that sample's location, is not shaded by one primitive throughout.
- It is computed from the test's own scene geometry, like `SlopeDepth` already does.
- `ShadeAt` takes the shade triangle's coverage and its `LESS_OR_EQUAL` test against the clear depth and the two slopes. Exact coverage ties within 1e-6, and depth ties within 1e-4, count as edges.
- Every pixel of a quad uses that one location. In both renders, the host quad that shades a sample holds every pixel at that sample's location, so the same rule describes both.
- The shade triangle moved to a namespace `Shade`, and `GuestLocation` is shared with `LocateDepth`.

**The compare.** `EdgeColorDifferences` replaces the exact path's color `Differences`:
- **Tolerated:** in an edge sample, channel 0 or 1 may sit one f16 step from the reference (adjacent f16 values: `AdjacentHalves`/`HalfStep`).
- **Still exact:** channels 2/3 (sample index), depth, the grid compare, and every interior sample stay bit for bit.

**Coverage of the rule.** Of the scene's 4096 samples, 288 are edge samples and 3808 stay bit-exact; I counted these with a Python mirror of the classifier. All four printed RDNA 4 samples classify as edge samples.

**Messages:**
- An untolerated difference prints the existing line unchanged: `color with the samples put in the guest's order: sample …`.
- A tolerated one prints `…, one f16 step in a barycentric of an edge sample: sample …`, up to 4 of each.
- The summary line adds `and by one f16 step in a barycentric of N edge samples`.
- When nothing was tolerated, the final line is unchanged: `…, bit for bit`.

**Limitation:** CruddOCE's log printed only the first 4 of the 42 differences. Whether the other 38 are all edge samples is unknown. If any interior sample differs on RDNA 4, the test still fails and now prints it with the existing message, which is the next thing to look at.

## Red / green

`agc_driver_sample_order_grid_reference` can't run here: lavapipe has no 2× color/depth targets ("skipped, the device has no 2x color and depth targets"). So the compare is checked without a device by a new mode, `agc_driver_sample_order_tests --edge-tolerance`, registered as the ctest `agc_driver_sample_order_edge_tolerance`.

**What the mode does:**
- It builds the scene's reference color analytically: f16-rounded barycentrics of whichever primitive shades each sample at its guest location, plus the sample index.
- It picks the first shaded edge sample and the first shaded interior sample programmatically. They came out as s1 of (33, 1) and s0 of (20, 14).
- It then requires:
  - the reference against itself: no difference;
  - the edge sample's second barycentric one step up, and its first one step up: tolerated;
  - the edge sample's second barycentric two steps up: a difference;
  - the edge sample's sample index one step up: a difference;
  - the interior sample's second barycentric one step up: a difference.
