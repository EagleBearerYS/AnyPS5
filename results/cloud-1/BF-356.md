## 5. #6926 and #13499: a host write over a read-trapped image

Line numbers are at the §4 local merge **1794a4ce6f** (e67cc81869 + #6926's d0b755a486) unless named otherwise.

**The host-write entry.** File, AIO and socket receives all bracket their copy with `GuestArena::HostWrite` (`libc/include/GuestArena.hpp:82–84`). That covers `libkernel/File/src/Stdio.cpp`, `libkernel/Aio/src/Aio.cpp`, `libSceNet/Export.cpp` and others. Its constructor calls `GuestArenaBeginHostWrite_nid_postfix` (`libc/src/GuestArena.cpp:727–735`):
1. **728:** the host-write hook, which is #6926's `GuestMemory::PrepareHostWrite`. The driver installs it at start-up (`Execution/src/Driver/Lifecycle.cpp:233`) and clears it at shutdown (160, 175), on every platform.
2. **732, non-Windows:** `GuestWriteWatch::GuestWriteWatchBeginHostWrite_nid_postfix`, which opens the pages.

**So #6926's store-and-wait runs first, always.**

**Step 1, #6926's store** (`Execution/src/GuestMemory.cpp:2807–2820`):
- **The call.** `PrepareHostWrite` sets `ReadSiteScope(ReadSite::Store)` (2812) and calls `FlushGpuWrites(address, bytes)` (2814). That goes to the active recorder's `FlushForAccess` (`Graphics/src/Recorder.cpp:1023`, installed at 1302).
- **The GPU sync.** `FlushForAccess` locks `GpuMutex` and syncs through the recorder's pending writes over the range (1040–1050).
- **The store.** It then calls `StorageTexture::FlushPending(…, PartialUnits, …)` (1072). It doesn't when the C4 rule keeps the images pending (1066–1070, below).
- **On a read-trapped image:**
  - `writeBack` widens the store to the whole image (`Graphics/src/Texture.cpp:6098–6101`).
  - `writeBackLayers` releases the trap when it returns, through the `ReleaseReadTrap` guard (6188–6191).
  - On the CPU round-trip path it releases traps on all sharing pages first (`releaseReadTraps(true)`, 6498).

  So the image's results are in guest memory, and its pages are untrapped, before the hook returns.
- **#13678 on the newest main** (§6) also adds read-trapped images nested inside a stored one to the same `FlushPending` (macOS only). This widens the same store.

**Step 2, the write watch** (`libc/src/GuestWriteWatch.cpp`, macOS `Watch`):
- **The bracket.** `BeginHostWrite` (459–471) runs inside `withoutTraps(begin, end, true, …)` (812–824).
- **Resolving what's left.** When any trap is armed (`_trapping`), `withoutTraps` first waits for other threads' resolutions and calls `resolve(begin, end)` (814–817). `resolve` takes every trap touching the range (`takeTraps`, 826–845), runs the resolver on it on this thread, and untraps it (759–776).
- **The resolver** is #13499's `ResolveLabelTrap` (`Texture.cpp:4292`). It does `FlushGpuWrites`, then waits while `DrawPipeline::HeldReads` (4293–4299).
- **Then, under the watch lock:** it re-checks that no trap or foreign resolution covers the range (819), untraps (820), records the host write in `_hostWrites` (465) and restores the pages' protection (466–469).

**Can the write fault?** No.
- The pages are open before the bytes land.
- `TrapReads` refuses a range overlapping a host write in flight (516–517), so `StoreReadbackBeforeLabel` (4769, trap at 4790) can't re-arm a trap until `EndHostWrite` (473–481).
- A store made inside the resolver can touch a page `resolve` has taken but not yet untrapped. Its write fault is handled as this thread's own resolution (`resolveTrap`, 699–702: `resolvingCovers(…, Owner::Self)` untraps the page).

**Can it land under a later store?** No, not of the results stored here.
- **Normal case:** step 1 stores the image and removes it from pending, so there's nothing left to store over the host's bytes.
- **The C4 case, kept by CPU:** `FlushForAccess` 1066–1070 keeps the images pending when every 64 KiB block of the access was written by the CPU since their generations. The image then stays pending and trapped through step 1. Step 2's resolver runs `FlushGpuWrites` again and hits the same C4 rule, then untraps the pages.
  - A later store of that image keeps the blocks the CPU wrote, which is what C4 relies on. On macOS `BeginHostWrite` marks the pages `Written` (468).
  - That is main's existing rule. #6926 doesn't change it, and I didn't verify it here.
  - The texture's `readTrapped` flag can stay set after the watch dropped the trap. The next label re-arms it, or the next store releases it (6098, 6188). That is harmless.

**Can it deadlock?** Not through #6926.
- `PrepareHostWrite` holds no lock across the hook and the write-watch step.
- **The one wait loop** is #13499's `ResolveLabelTrap` (`while (HeldReads(…)) sleep 50 µs`). It is reached from step 2 only when a trap is still on the range after step 1, which in practice means the C4 case.
  - If that host write is made while holding `GuestMemory::GpuMutex`, the loop could wait on work that needs the mutex.
  - #6926 counts and logs that case (`noteLockedHostWrite`, 2738–2746), and `APS5_ASSERT_GPU_LOCK=1` aborts on it.
  - The same loop is reached without #6926, at main's own `BeginHostWrite`. #6926 makes it rarer, since step 1 usually stores the image first.

**Linux.**
- **The trap paths are all macOS-only, so none of them exists on Linux:**
  - `Texture.cpp`: `ResolveLabelTrap` and `LabelTrapsEnabled` (4284–4305), the trap branch of `StoreReadbackBeforeLabel` (4776–4786, 4789–4794), and `releaseReadTraps` (6074–6094) are `#if defined(__APPLE__)`;
  - the `GuestWriteWatch` wrappers are no-ops or return false off macOS: `BeginHostWrite`/`EndHostWrite` at 1052–1070, `SetReadTrapResolver` at 1137–1144, `TrapReads` at 1146–1154, `ReleaseReads` at 1156–1163.
- **What is compiled on Linux but never used:** the `readTrapped` member and `writeBack`'s widening (6098–6101). `readTrapped` is only set at 4791, inside the macOS block.
- **#6926's `PrepareHostWrite` is compiled and active on Linux.** It is the only one of the two that runs there.
