## Short answer

- **F1 works and is safe to land with F6.** Aligned power-of-two blocks turn 64 adjacent 64 KiB pieces into 1 import (main: 64), a window with its last piece missing into 7 (main: 64), and the 48-piece unaligned run into 5 (main: 48). A synthetic Village-like registry of 30,000 pieces needs 8,872 imports instead of 30,000.
- **F1 alone multiplied the refusal storm; I fixed that inside F1.** My first F1 stepped a refused block down one size at a time. At a full budget that tried up to 8 sizes per range: 83,769 refusals per build on the 30,000-piece registry, and Upload went to 4-7 s. A budget refusal now steps straight to the largest block that fits what is left, and stops when not even the range fits: a full budget costs one refusal per request, as on main.
- **F2 is not needed (step 2).** F1 alone makes T3 green: a 4 MiB storage image over 64 pieces now finds its block import (1 import; main: 64 segment imports). At +64 KiB it takes 2 segment imports (4 MiB + 128 KiB), inside the 7 or fewer GAC-024 expects. `importSegments` is untouched.
- **F6 stops the walk's retries.** With a registry of 12,000 ranges and a budget for 60 % of them, every rebuild re-tried all 4,800 refused ranges before; with F6 it tries 0 until the budget frees, and every remembered refusal still counts as a refusal. It also keeps blocks that were stepped down for the budget across registry changes.
- **What rebuilds the space** (step 3): any registry generation change and any import retire (`state.epoch`). F7a, an epoch scoped to the space's own imports, is **proposed, not built**: it is small in lines but not safe as GAC-024 sketched it (§ Rebuilds).
- **F3 is built, with the 04:43 rule**, and I recommend it as **its own PR** after the first (it depends on F6's budget comparison). The `maxMemoryAllocationCount` cap is **not built**: NVIDIA's Windows driver reports 4,096 while Village held 27,167 live imports without a failure.

## F1: aligned power-of-two blocks (commit 4)

**What changed** (GuestBufferMemory.cpp):
- `importRun(lease, address, largest)`: one outward scan for the continuing extent inside the aligned 8 MiB window, then binary searches for the largest aligned block (from 2 × the alignment up to the window, at most `largest`) that holds the address and is exactly tiled by continuing readable ranges. Per call: O(extent) + 7 × O(log extent), not 7 × 128 (the Bug Finder's "one pass").
- **Step-downs in `importRunAt`:**
  - a budget refusal steps to the largest block that fits `budget + evictable − live`, and stops when not even the address's own range fits;
  - a held import in the block steps down one size;
  - a failed block (a refused Windows alias, `vkAllocateMemory`, dma-buf) is remembered in `failedBlocks` (block and its owners) until one of its ranges changes, and the next smaller block is tried. `unaliasedSpans` (the permanent single-range pinning) is gone.
- **The walk skips covered ranges:** a range inside the import the walk just took reuses it without `importAllocation`; the bounds are copied under the lock, so a retire between iterations (another thread, with pipelining) never leaves a dangling read.
- `ClearHostImports` and a device change clear `failedBlocks`; map-time failure forgetting also erases the range's own entry.
- `sameRange` stays tied to `importRun` (I tried tying it to the import's own ranges: that broke the growth of held imports at refresh, `agc_host_import_retire` "the grown run was not imported as one import replacing the first", and was reverted).

**Bug F1 exposed in a test, fixed as commit 3:** `agc_driver_storage_write_back_overhang` read both surfaces straight from guest memory after `FlushAllPending` + `WaitIdle`. A write-back through an import is only recorded; a reader calls `FlushGpuWrites` (GuestMemory.hpp says so). It passed on main only because the split surface took the CPU write-back, whose `SubmitAndWait` also drained the first surface's recorded store (instrumented: after the first write, main's surface held 0 texels too). With F1 the split surface's two ranges form one 128 KiB block, nothing submits, and it failed 3 of 3 (`the fully registered surface received no texels`). With `FlushGpuWrites` it passes 10 of 10 on F1 and on main.

## F6: remembered budget refusals (commit 5)

- **Remembered:** in `importRunAt`'s refusal branch, a request that may not evict (the walk, map time) records the refused run (`refusals`, keyed by base and size, with its owners) and the live bytes and budget at the refusal. When the chain stops, the address's own range is recorded too.
- **Answered:** `importAllocation` checks the address's own range first (one binary search and one map lookup, before `importRun`). A refusal still stands while live bytes have not dropped below the recorded value, the budget has not grown (after F3: `budget <= recorded`) and the run's ranges are the same. It is counted as a refusal and as `remembered`.
- **Skipped blocks:** `importRun(…, budget)` skips remembered blocks, so `sameRange` keeps a block stepped down for the budget (before F6 it was retired and imported again at every registry change).
- **Forgotten:** a new import drops the refusals of runs it covers; a generation change drops stale or changed ones; a device change and `ClearHostImports` clear them.
- **Requests that may evict** (lookups, `UploadFinish`'s per-region lookup) never consult the memory.

**The Bug Finder's contract:**
1. A remembered refusal is still a refusal: `refused` (+ `refusedBytes`) moves for it, so an `overBudget` test on `refused` sees it. `remembered` counts the subset; `refused − remembered` is the number of real tries (the test hook).
2. The evict-and-retry path is never blocked: the memory is consulted only for `evict = false`.
3. Freeing budget ends the memory: every retire lowers `liveBytes` below the recorded value, and a larger budget ends it too.
- **How R3 / BF-010 sit on top:** R3 calls `importAllocation(…, evict = true)` after a build whose walk moved `refused` (remembered refusals included), so it runs exactly when it does today. Its evictions lower `liveBytes`, which ends the memory, so the next walk tries again. BF-010 widens when R3 runs; the same three points hold.

## What rebuilds the address space, and F7a (step 3, second part)

- The cached space (GBM `AcquireRegistered`) is rebuilt when (a) the registry generation moved (any mutation, GPU range or not) or (b) `state.epoch` moved. `retireImport` moves the epoch for every retire: refresh-time retires, replacements, `retireLeastRecent` evictions by lookups at a full budget, and #4448's idle retires. `UploadFinish`'s dissolve check sends a cached space down the per-build path on the same epoch.
- At a full budget every lookup that needs room evicts, so each eviction forces the next address-based build to rebuild: GAC-024 §8's effect 2. F1 + F6 make each rebuild cheaper (fewer imports to reconcile, covered ranges skipped, refusals answered from memory) but not rarer.
- **F7a (proposed, not built).** Scope the cache's epoch to imports the space references (a per-import marker and a counter `retireImport` bumps only for marked imports; the two checks compare it).
  - **Why not here:** a space that is no longer current can still be in flight. With pipelining that is the committer thread's build, while the worker makes the next one; its regions hold import pointers too. A marker for the *current* space misses retires of an older space's imports, and its dissolve check would then trust a dangling pointer.
  - **Doing it safely** needs a per-import reference count that every live space holds, decremented when the space is destroyed. That destructor runs wherever the last `shared_ptr` drops, so it has to take the imports lock from arbitrary threads.
  - So it's not "small and safe". **Recommended as its own PR** with GAC-024's T6: a retire outside the space is a hit; retiring a space import still rebuilds; plus an in-flight-old-space case.

## F3: the budget from the device (commit 6)

- **The device's budget:** `HostImportBudgetFor(memory, reported, configured, live)`, a pure function as #6174's `DeviceBudget`. For the largest non-device-local heap with a reported budget: `heapBudget − (heapUsage − min(heapUsage, live)) − heapBudget / 8`, rounded down to 64 MiB, at most the configured budget. The effective budget reads `VK_EXT_memory_budget` at most once a second (`context.memoryProperties2`, set when the device enables the extension).
- **The adaptive ceiling, with the 04:43 rule:** a `vkAllocateMemory` failure of an import lowers the budget to the live bytes only when it came within an eighth of the budget or is the third in a row (a success resets the run). An isolated failure further below only marks its range failed (the existing `failed`/`failedBlocks`). It prints once: `[gpu] host import budget lowered to the 1234.5 MiB live after vkAllocateMemory failed within an eighth of the budget`. The ceiling holds until the device or the configured budget changes.
- **With F6:** a refusal stays remembered while the budget doesn't grow, so a falling device budget keeps the memory and a rising one ends it.
- **Not built: the cap at `maxMemoryAllocationCount`.** `BufferPool` takes at most a sixth of it per tier (half in all). NVIDIA's Windows driver reports 4,096, and Village (arm B) held 27,167 live imports with 0 `vkAllocateMemory` failures, so a cap at the reported limit would refuse most of them. That needs the PC's measurement of where NVIDIA really fails first.
- **#6174 is still open** (d6b002887c, not on upstream/main ff23657f3), so the query is local. Once #6174 lands, `HostImportBudgetFor` can share its reading pattern; their formulas differ (device-local heap ÷ 8 against host heap less others' usage).

## Pipelining (addendum 03:46)

- **Requesters on the draw-commit thread:** yes. With pipelined draws (main's default), `Draw.cpp` enqueues the draw's commit and `Driver::commitDraw` runs `VulkanDevice::Draw` on the DrawPipeline committer thread under the GPU mutex. The address-based build (walk, R2), its Upload pass (R5) and the draw's lookups (access, segment, join at bind) now run there instead of on the queue worker.
- **Elsewhere:**
  - map-time imports (R1) still run on the GPU-map observer;
  - dispatches drain the pipeline first (`Dispatch.cpp:87`), so a dispatch's build never interleaves with a pipelined draw's.
- **Lock order:** unchanged. It is GPU mutex, then the imports lock, as on the worker. Map time still takes only the imports lock and refuses to retire without the GPU mutex.
- **`pointerHeld`:** unchanged. `buildFloor` is set per address-based build, and builds are serialized by the GPU mutex, wherever they run.
- **What the fix adds for it:**
  - the walk's covered-range skip reads only bounds saved under the lock, because worker lookups can now retire imports between two of the committer's lock sections;
  - F1's held step-down uses the same `pointerHeld`.

## Adversarial review (GAC-024 §4.6, updated)

1. **"Blocks pin memory nobody asked for."** Only registered GPU-readable ranges inside one aligned block, which the walk imports anyway. Under a tight budget a block steps down to what fits, so it never refuses what a single range would get.
2. **"Remaps inside a block retire more."** Yes: a remap of one piece retires its block, and the next use imports the largest tiled block. Bounded by the remap rate.
3. **"Held imports block upgrades."** The block steps down while held. At the next generation the smaller import fails `sameRange` and is retired at refresh, and the walk then imports the block: main's growth path, kept on purpose.
4. **"Windows aliases of a block can fail."** The block steps down, and the failure is remembered per block until its ranges change; no session-long single-range pinning. The Windows variant needs the PC.
5. **"F1 multiplies refusals."** The first version did: 83,769 refusals per build and 7x main's Upload time. The room-based step-down caps a full budget at one refusal per request (measured: 24,000, as main).
6. **"F6 hides freed budget."** The memory holds only while `liveBytes ≥` the value at the refusal and the budget hasn't grown; every retire lowers `liveBytes`, and a new import forgets what it covers.
   - **Edge:** a lookup can evict X to import a sub-block of a remembered block without `liveBytes` falling below the recorded value. The larger block then stays skipped until the next drop. That is a missed upgrade, never a wrong import.
7. **"F6 blocks evict-and-retry."** No: only `evict = false` requests consult it (tested with a lookup of a remembered range).
8. **"F6's memory grows."** One entry per refused run: at most about two per refused range, since blocks are shared. Entries go at the next generation change once stale or changed, and when an import covers them.
9. **"F3 lowers the budget on a transient failure."** Only within an eighth of the budget or on the third failure in a row; an isolated failure stays per-range (tested). The ceiling holds until the device or the configured budget changes, so one near-budget transient keeps the budget there for the session: accepted, and visible in the log line.
10. **"A memory-budget report can be stale, 0 or wrong."**
    - 0 or no report keeps the configured budget. The report is re-read once a second and rounded to 64 MiB so small usage changes don't end F6's memory.
    - A host heap reported over budget gives 0, so every import is refused and copied: safe, slow.
    - The formula assumes the driver counts imports in `heapUsage`; if it doesn't, live above usage is treated as zero others.
    - NVIDIA's reported host-heap budget is unverified (WINDOWS).
11. **"F1 re-imports more bytes while a window fills or shrinks."** Yes, with builds between the pieces: 16 MiB imported against 4 MiB for a 4 MiB run filled piece by piece, and 7 MiB against 0 when its start moves up to 2 MiB (§ Addendum 08:26 PT). It is bounded by the window, and parl0v's carry-over would cut it to the new part.
12. **"F1 turns compared imports into settled ones on Windows."** Blocks of 1 MiB or more fall under #6060's settle rule: one blocking settle round trip each when made, then watched instead of compared. WINDOWS should show the count.
13. **Not covered here:**
    - Windows: aliases of large blocks, heap mirrors, NVIDIA's reported host-heap budget, T5's alias variant;
    - R5's per-build evict tries on Linux copies;
    - F7a.
