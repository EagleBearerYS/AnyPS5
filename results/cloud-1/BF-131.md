## The new hop kind and the depth rule

**The hop kind.**
- `PointerChase` (in `ControlFlowInfo.hpp`, next to `PointerHop`) holds `{offset, immediate, readOffset, readImmediate}`:
  - the V# is the 4 dwords at `ScalarAddressDword(P, offset, immediate + 4 * i)`;
  - the next pointer is the 2 dwords at `base + ScalarBufferDword(readOffset, readImmediate (+4), V# size)`.
- `TableColumn` gains `pointerChases` (the distinct kinds) and `pointerChaseDepth`.
- The disk cache serialises both. Its format goes 46 → 47, and the encoder's layout asserts are updated (TableColumn 120 → 144 bytes, 20 → 22 fields; DescriptorSource 200 → 224).
- Plan validation rejects a decoded column whose chases and depth disagree, whose depth is over 4, or which has chases without being pointer-keyed.

**Arms (tracker, `PointerRecordPairs`).**
- The walk over the merged pointer's (low, high) Phi edges is unchanged: edge pairing, lane-mask and constant words skipped, masked words.
- It also takes a chase arm. That is a low word read by `ReadConstBuffer` at an immediate offset through a `GetBufferResource`, whose four words `LoadAddressU32` reads at consecutive immediates from one `GetAddressResource` (the V# at P + 0x60 in the tester's shape).
- The high word must be the same handle's read at the next dword. A high-word Phi is walked arm by arm.
- Each distinct buffer handle is a chase site.

**Depth.**
- After the merged pointer's walk, each site's V# pointer is walked the same way. It must resolve to the record pointer R or to other chase sites, which gives the site graph.
- depth(site) = 1 + the deepest site its pointer reaches; a pointer that reaches only R counts 0.
- The column's depth is the deepest site among the merged pointer's arms. A site that reaches itself (a Phi loop) is refused, and so is a depth over `MaxPointerChaseDepth` = 4.
- #9457's buffer-handle refusal is unchanged, since chases are only taken for pointer-keyed columns.
- A high word that is a chase read on an edge whose low word is the record word, the unpaired fallback, is accepted only if its handle is a known site.

**The tester's shape:** H1 (V# from R + 0x60) has depth 1. H2 (V# from Phi(R, H1) + 0x60) has depth 2. The column has one chase kind {0, 0x60, 0, 0} and depth 2.

## The closure's read order (materializer, `ReadColumn`, indirect branch)

1. As before, every record is read in key order: pointer R from the record's dwords at the column offset, then #9457's hops, then the T# or S# at the column's descriptor offset. Each R is recorded as the entry's key.
2. The frontier starts as the distinct captured record pointers, in record order.
3. For depth 1 to `pointerChaseDepth`, for each pointer P in the frontier and each chase kind in discovery order:
   - read the 4 V# words at P + offset + immediate (+4 each). Each must be 4-aligned and in the snapshot, and each is added to the snapshot ranges, so a later write to it is seen;
   - decode the V# as the driver does (base from dwords 0/1, size from dword 2). A V# that is not a buffer ends this chase;
   - read the low and high words at base + `ScalarBufferDword(readOffset, readImmediate)` and the next dword. They must lie inside the V# size and in the snapshot, and are also added to the ranges;
   - the next pointer is high:low. A null pointer, or one already captured (record or earlier closure entry), is kept once and not expanded again;
   - a new pointer becomes a new pointer-keyed entry. #9457's hops are followed from it and its T# or S# is read at its own descriptor offset. An unreadable T# poisons that entry as unmapped.
   - The new pointers form the next frontier.
4. Entries stop at the column's key limit (65536). Past it the column is marked outside.
5. A chase that cannot be read adds no entry, so a GPU that reaches that pointer misses in the binary search and faults "outside the snapshot".

**Data.**
- Records 0..3 hold pointers to nodes 2, 0, 1 and 2.
- Each node holds a T# at +0 (images F, O, F, O) and a V# at +0x60 whose buffer's dwords 0/1 point to the next node: 0→1, 1→2, 2→0 and 3→3.
- Group g takes chase step k when bit k of g is set (`s_bitcmp1_b32 s18, k`), so the 4 groups cover none, the first only, the second only and both.
- Lanes 0-31 and 32-63 take records {1,2}, {2,1}, {1,1} and {2,2}.
- The nodes and links are registered with `GuestAllocations`, like `RunPointerSamplerTests`' records. Without that, the chase reads that the GPU executes itself crashed llvmpipe, a SIGSEGV in JIT code.

**Cases:**
- **All 16 lane classes** (4 groups × 2 records × the taken steps) read the T# of the node they reach.
- **Moved link:** node 0's link is moved to node 3, which is otherwise unreached, without a recompile (same `artifactId`), and every group's results follow.
- **Link outside the snapshot:** node 1's V# points at a link buffer that is registered for the GPU but not in the snapshot regions. It faults "outside the snapshot" with the output untouched, and does not recompile:
  `[gpu] deferred write-back failed: image table: shader 0x561c88a5ec00 stage 4 pc 0xcc selected the descriptor at 0x7ffee6be8200: outside the snapshot` (the address is node 3's).
- **Refused, 58 dwords:** a chase repeated in a counted loop (`PointerChaseLoopCode`). Its site's pointer is a Phi of R and itself.
- **Refused, 87 dwords:** five conditional chase steps (`PointerChaseDeepCode`, depth 5 > 4).
- Both refusals are checked as "not a valid runtime value", the way the nested-table refusal tests check theirs.
- I proved the refusals come from the new rules:
  - with the cap raised to 5, the five-step shader is accepted ("five conditional chase steps was not refused");
  - with the cycle check disabled, the loop is accepted ("a chase repeated in a loop was not refused").

## Notes

- **The sampler wall (`ShaderInfo::MaxSamplers` = 32):** the synthetic shader cannot show it. It has one sampler, from the SRT. The closure only adds entries to the table map at snapshot time. It creates no image or sampler resource, so it does not change the compiled sampler count; the wall the tester saw comes from elsewhere in that shader.
- **Unpaired joins:** where the low and high Phis are in different blocks, a chase arm's high word must be its own read, or a chase read of a known site under the record's low word. The tester saw one such join. If it pairs a chase low word with the record's high word, it is still refused. Their IR would show which.
- No game data was used.
