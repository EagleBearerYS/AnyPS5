## The fix

It follows the recompiler's result memo (`Recompiler.cpp`: `resultBytes`/`vectorBytes`, `heldBytes`, `APS5_RESULT_MEMO_MB` → `resultMemoBudgetBytes`, `trimResultMemo` trimming to ¾ of the budget):

- **Charge at insert:** `HeapWalkMemo::remember(key, entry)` sets `entry.bytes = HeapWalkEntryBytes(entry)` and adds it to `heldBytes`. `HeapWalkEntryBytes` counts `sizeof(HeapWalkEntry)`, the capacity of the entry's vectors (`userData`, `ranges`, `values`, `accessible`, `userDataUse.reads`), and what its result, capture and words pin (`resultBytes`, `captureBytes`, `wordsBytes`).
  - These byte helpers are local copies in `HeapStages.cpp`, built from BF-254's accounting (21284736e9).
  - There is no call into `ShaderRecompiler::OwnedBytes`, and no per-distinct-pointer scan.
- **Credit at drop:** every path that drops an entry subtracts its charge:
  - the fifth way (`pop_back`);
  - the two previous-generation keys `slot()` drains per call;
  - the whole old `previous` when the 16,384-key cap rotates `current` into it;
  - a trim.
- **Budget, both generations:** after an insert, if `heldBytes > budget`, `trim()` runs:
  1. It drops the whole `previous` generation first.
  2. If the memo is still over budget, it drops the oldest-stored entries of `current` until it is back under ¾ of the budget. The entries carry an insertion stamp `stored`. A key whose ways all go is erased.
  3. The dropped entries are destroyed, which releases their results, captures and words.
- **Env override:** `APS5_HEAP_WALK_MEMO_MB` (MiB, like `APS5_RESULT_MEMO_MB`), default 256; `0` keeps only the key cap. It has a row in `docs/dev/ENVIRONMENT.md`. The budget is per memo, so per thread (`HostThreadLocal`).
- **Unchanged:** the 16,384-key cap, the admit rule (`seen`), the hit path (`find`, `sameWalkInputs`, way rotation) and the walk. `rememberWalk` now fills a local entry and hands it to `remember`. It stores the same fields in the same way order as before.
- **Shader cache guard:** I touched nothing under `core/shader/**`, `HostThreadLocal.hpp` or `AtomicSharedPtr.hpp`. The new header and `HeapStages.cpp` make no `ShaderRecompiler::…(` call, and the guard check passes in configure.

## The budget default: 256 MiB per memo

- **Same scale as the other caches:** it equals the result memo's default (`APS5_RESULT_MEMO_MB=256`, which holds ~240 MiB in practice) and the heap template budget (`APS5_HEAP_TEMPLATE_MIB=256`). That keeps this cache in the same range as the recompiler's own caches.
- **In Village:** BF-254 saw one walking thread, so the memo is bounded at 256 MiB instead of the 7,400 MiB it reached. Its 3,752 MiB of growth was 81 % of the 4,639 MB private-memory climb. The bound also keeps the memo near the size it had at the menu, where a lookup took 2 µs, against 120 µs at 7.4 GB.
- **Worst case:** threads × 256 MiB. A smaller default would cut the hit rate further (see below). A larger one moves back towards the climb. `APS5_HEAP_WALK_MEMO_MB` tunes it per run.

## Red / green

The test is `agc_driver_heap_walk_memo` (`core/libs/prx/libSceAgcDriver/tests/HeapWalkMemo.cpp`). It is a pure unit test: no Vulkan device, it only links `libSceAgcDriver`, so it runs on lavapipe and on the GPU-less Windows runner.

1. **Oldest entries go:** it stores 64 entries of known size (16 KiB of `values` plus an empty result each) under a budget of 16 entries.
   - After every insert it requires the bytes the memo holds (summed over both generations) to equal its charge and stay within the budget.
   - At the end it requires the oldest entries gone and their results released (`weak_ptr` expired), and the newest findable with its result.
   - It also checks that an entry's charge is exactly its struct plus its vectors' capacity.
2. **Previous generation first:** it fills 16,385 tiny keys so that the key cap starts a generation, then stores one 8 MiB entry over the budget.
   - It requires the previous generation dropped, both current entries kept, and the memo within budget.

## What a smaller memo may cost in hit rate (from the code)

- **What a hit saves:** `MaterializeHeapStage` returns the memo's result after a key lookup, `sameWalkInputs` (a compare of the entry's guest ranges with memory) and `rangesSettled`. A miss runs the SRT walk (`invocation.Capture`), `MaterializeHeap` and the insert. BF-254 measured 2 µs per lookup and 11 µs per heap stage call at the menu, when the memo was small.
- **What the cap drops:** with ~307 KiB per entry in Village, 256 MiB holds ~850 entries (~640 right after a trim to ¾). The uncapped memo held 24,143 entries over 10,638 keys. Every use of a dropped entry pays a miss again.
  - If the scene's per-frame set of distinct stage inputs is larger than what fits, each excess input costs about one walk (~10 µs) per frame instead of a lookup.
  - The cap only makes this worse if the trimmed entries would have been hit again. Uncapped, they made every lookup slower (120 µs) instead.
- **Trim order is by insertion, not by last hit.** The hit path is unchanged as asked, so a hit doesn't refresh an entry's stamp. A hot entry stored long ago is trimmed once its stamp is among the oldest, then walked and stored again on its next use.
  - Re-entry is immediate: the key's `seen` slot normally still holds it, and `memoKnown` is true if the key kept other ways. So a hot entry costs one extra walk per budget turnover, not one per frame.
  - Stamping on hit (LRU, one assignment in the hit loop) would avoid that. I left it out because the task keeps the hit path unchanged.
- **The charge overstates the memo's own memory a little:** an entry is charged its whole result, capture and words even when another holder shares them. BF-254 found 5,851 of 7,400 MiB memo-only, so the effective budget is somewhat tighter than its number.
- **Next step for a real number:** BF-254's `[gpu-heap] walk memo` counters on `diag/9508-walk-memo-bytes`, with a hit/miss count added, in one Village run per budget (e.g. 128/256/512 MiB).
