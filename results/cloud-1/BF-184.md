## Which negative case changes

- **The task names the wrong case.** It expects "the leaf whose short operand sits right before the exit" to frame. On a376e37b88 that leaf stays refused, and a different one frames.
- **Why "before the exit" stays refused** (StoreBelowRsp, LoadAtRsp, then the exit's pops):
  - The forward extension runs first. It takes the exit's first `pop r15`, which is `Sequential` and not a free, so the stub reaches 6 bytes.
  - The backward extension therefore never runs.
  - The widened stub overlaps the exit site, so `overlapsSite` refuses it, as before.
- **What frames instead:** the case "operand at rsp before a rip-relative load". Its forward extension stops at the rip-relative instruction, and the backward extension takes the store below rsp before it: 9 bytes.
- **The commit, accordingly:**
  - moves that leaf into a new positive case, `stubsOperandsAtRspWithTheInstructionsBeforeThem`;
  - asserts its stub: from the store, 9 bytes, body `48 89 7c 24 00 48 8b 44 24 10` + jmp, 0 in-place moves, 2 stubbed operands;
  - keeps "before the exit" with the EVEX, branch-target and short-prologue leaves in `skipsLeavesWithOperandsThatCannotMove` (4 refused, 2 of them "no room").
- **A possible follow-up, not in this branch:** make the forward extension stop at an exit site's first instruction. Then "before the exit" would frame through the backward extension too.

## Double counting and the shift

- **A prior in `moves`: yes, it happens.** A backward prior that is a movable rsp operand was already put in `moves` by the main scan.
  - **Why it's safe:** the extension lowers `stub.First` to the prior, so the `moves.erase` loop over `First..Last` drops it.
  - **Without that,** its in-place displacement patch would land inside the stub site, over the jump.
  - **Covered by:** the static variant (its `add rax,[rsp-0x20]` prior is in the stub, and no displacement falls inside either stub site, 5 moved in total). In the execution harness, `load()` would fail "a framed site's bytes changed before it was patched".
- **A prior inside an earlier stub: refused, not double-covered.** The extension can step back into the previous stub's range only when that range ends right at this stub's start. The widened range then overlaps that stub, so `overlapsSite`, which includes earlier stubs, refuses the leaf.
- **The prologue, the exits and the free:**
  - `stub.First > p` keeps the extension out of the prologue.
  - Exits can't be reached, because the instruction before them is `ret` or the extension stops at the free (`frees.contains(j)`). They are in `overlapsSite` anyway.
  - **So no extra guard is needed.**
- **The shift:**
  - **A prior that is an rsp operand:** re-encoded with `use.RspDisp + shift`, the same as the forward extension and the in-place moves, and `++stub.Operands`.
  - **Seen in the bytes:** the static variant's stub is `48 03 44 24 00` (`add rax,[rsp-0x20]` moved by the shift of 0x20) then `48 39 7c 24 20` (the `cmp` at rsp moved to +0x20). `StubbedOperands` is 3 over the two leaves (1 + 2).
  - **A non-rsp prior** (`add %rdi,%r8`) is copied verbatim, and `Operands` stays 1.
- **Branch targets:** `targetInside` refuses targets strictly inside (Begin, End).
  - A loop head at the prior, which becomes `stub.Begin`, is still allowed: the static and execution variant's `jne` back to its `add rax,[rsp-0x20]`.
  - A target at the `cmp` is refused: the refusal case, "a cmp at rsp whose loop branches back to it".
