# GAC-048: storage-image cache entry cap rebased onto main, tested, and read against the open storage-cache PRs

## The three answers

- **(a) Is the cap on by default, and at what value?** Yes, at **2048 entries**.
  - `storageEntries()` (ShaderResources.cpp:648 at the new head) reads `APS5_STORAGE_CACHE_ENTRIES` once. Unset or `0` gives 2048.
  - The eviction loop in `cachedStorageTexture` (:1301) evicts the least recently used surface while the cache holds that many entries, so the cache holds at most 2048 after each insert.
  - At Lana's rate of about 50 new surfaces a minute, 1,017 surfaces after 5.5 minutes reaches 2,048 after about 20.6 more minutes ((2048 − 1017) / 50).

- **(c) Does main or any open PR already bound the number of cached storage images?** **No.**
  - **Main** (73f4f7e06e) bounds the storage-image cache by bytes only:

### How the entry cap and #6194 compose

**What #6194 does** (`ShaderResources.cpp` at 8bb1300680):
- **Counting:** `cachedStorageTexture` evicts while `cache.bytes + entry.accounted > budget`, and increments `storageBudgetEvictions` for **every pass through that loop** (:994-996).
- **Growing:** `storageBudget` reads the device budget once a second. If 16 or more evictions happened since the last read and the heap has room (`usage + step + reserve <= reportedBudget`), `NextStorageBudget` (:1053) grows the byte budget by `step = max(current/8, 64 MiB)`, up to `storageBudgetCap`.
- **Shrinking:** while `usage + reserve > reportedBudget`, it shrinks by a step, down to the floor.

**The two limits bound different things:**
- The entry cap bounds **how many** surfaces the cache holds (at most 2048).
- #6194's budget bounds **how many bytes** the cache holds, and moves that bound between a floor and a cap.
- With both active, an insert evicts least-recently-used entries until both hold. Small surfaces hit the count first; large ones hit the bytes first.

**Does an entry-count eviction count as "evicting under pressure" in #6194's code?** Only if a merge puts it in the same loop body.
- #6194 counts every pass through its eviction loop. On the branch, that loop's condition is `bytes > budget || entries >= cap`. A merge that keeps #6194's `{ evict; storageBudgetEvictions++ }` body under the combined condition counts entry-count evictions too.
- **The Lana-like case that follows:** a title creating about 50 new small surfaces a minute past the 2048 limit yields fewer than 16 such evictions per one-second read. So the growth rule (16 or more) would not fire from that alone.
- **A burst can trip it:** a load or scene change that makes 16 or more new surfaces within a second, at the cap, does trip it. That grows the byte budget by a step while the cache is far under its bytes.
- **Growing would not help:** the count, not the bytes, is what evicts there.
- **The right merge** counts only the evictions where the byte condition held: test `cache.bytes + entry.accounted > budget` separately inside the loop, and increment only then. #6194's counter then keeps meaning "the byte budget is too small".
- **Rebase first:** #6194 already conflicts with main's `storageBudget`, which main rewrote since #6194's base (64ffeb8c9f, 39c23664bd, b996e31d8c, 0d6a6f96ee). So it needs a rebase before this question is real.

**What each leaves unbounded:**
- **#6194 alone:** the number of cached surfaces. Small surfaces at new addresses never reach the byte budget, and the budget may even grow. Each holds Vulkan objects, a sampled view and CPU memory that `heldBytes` doesn't count.
- **The entry cap alone:** nothing new on the byte side; main's byte budget stays as it is.
  - **The thrash risk:** if one frame touches more than 2048 distinct storage surfaces, least-recently-used eviction at insert can evict a surface the same frame uses again, which is then recreated.
  - #12786 protects the current present's storage images only in its allocation-failure recovery (544cbf2044), not in this insert loop.
- **Both:** images evicted from the cache but still referenced elsewhere stay alive until those holders let go.
  - A recording batch can hold them: `recorder->KeepBytes` and `Keep` of the dependent `ShaderResources`, ShaderResources.cpp:1314-1319 at the head.
  - So can a `ShaderResources` that bound them.
  - Neither limit counts these. They are bounded only by the batches in flight.
