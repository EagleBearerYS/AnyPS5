## Commit 1: the high word at its own immediate

- `PointerChase` (ControlFlowInfo.hpp) gains `readHighImmediate`. It is part of the defaulted `operator==`, so a column's equality and the disk cache comparison both cover it.
- New `ChaseHighRead` in ResourceTracker.cpp takes the edge's high-word read through `ChaseRead`. It accepts the read only when all of these hold:
  - the read's V# is the low read's handle, or an `EquivalentValue` of it;
  - its buffer offset equals `chase.readOffset`;
  - its immediate is 4-aligned and differs from `chase.readImmediate`.

  The result goes into `chase.readHighImmediate` before the site is looked up. The old rule (high immediate = low + 4) is now just one case of this.
- ResourceMaterializer.cpp: `Chase()` reads the next pointer's high word with `ScalarBufferDword(chase.readOffset, chase.readHighImmediate, size)`.
- ShaderDiskCache.cpp: the encoder and decoder store `readHighImmediate` after `readImmediate`. A decoded column is refused when any chase has an unaligned high immediate or one equal to the low immediate. ShaderDiskCache.hpp: `FormatVersion` goes 47 → 48. The cache tests use `FormatVersion - 1` and needed no change.
- docs/dev/debt/shader.md, line 45: the chase sentence now says each word is read "at its own fixed immediate of the scalar buffer load (the high word need not follow the low one)". It also says the high word must be read "through that arm's V# at that arm's buffer offset".
  - I wrote "one V# at one buffer offset" rather than "one load", because the check is per V# and offset, not per instruction.
  - Two `s_buffer_load_dword`s through the same V# at the same offset are therefore accepted. That matches what the materializer reads.

### Test: `agc_driver_pointer_chase_split_table` (`--pointer-chase-split`)

This is hand-written RDNA (`PointerChaseSplitCode`, 73 words, assembled with llvm-mc gfx1030) in ImageTable.cpp next to `RunPointerChaseTests`. It is registered in both `set_tests_properties` lists after `agc_driver_pointer_chase_table`.

It reproduces Rift Apart's shape:
- a T# at a record pointer;
- up to two chase steps through a V# at the pointer plus 0x60;
- a third chase step through a V# at the pointer plus 0x110, whose next pointer is dword 5 (0x54) and dword 11 (0x6c) of one `s_buffer_load_dwordx16` from 0x40.

The setup:
- Eight nodes; the chase steps per group are {7, 4, 3, 5} (bit 0 and bit 1 are near steps, bit 2 is the far step).
- The graph is built so node 6 is reachable only by near, near, far. A plan that is one step short, or that reads the far link's high word at the wrong immediate, therefore samples a node outside the snapshot rather than an earlier node.
- Checks:
  1. every taken and skipped step;
  2. a moved far link (`linkFar(0,2)`, artifactId unchanged);
  3. the link restored;
  4. a far link to an unsnapshotted node, which must fault "outside the snapshot".

Three refusal controls run first, so the red tree reaches them too:
- `PointerChaseSplitHandleCode` (77 words): the high word through a different V#.
- `PointerChaseSplitOffsetCode` (76 words): the high word at another `readOffset`.
- `PointerChaseSplitWordCode` (73 words): the high word at the same immediate as the low word.

Each must be refused ("not a valid runtime value"), and is refused on both trees.

## Commit 2: one chase site per V# word pair

The problem: `PointerRecordPairs`' site lookup matched on the handle alone. A second chase arm that reads its next pointer through the same V# at other words therefore reused the first arm's site and its `PointerChase`. The second link was never added to the column or to the snapshot's closure.

The fix: sites are now found by the handle (or an `EquivalentValue` one) together with the whole `PointerChase`.
