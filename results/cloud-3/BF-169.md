## Every scalar-address path, and what changed

Line numbers are at the head.

**Changed: these could read the base's low bits.**

1. **The emitter, handle-pair path:** `GuestAddressBase`, `core/shader/recompiler/SpirvBackend/src/SpirvMemory/SpirvMemoryInstructions.cpp:179-199`.
   - For `ResourceKind::ScalarAddress`, the low word of the SGPR base pair (`handle` argument 0) is now ANDed with `~3` before `DeviceAddressFromWords` (`:193-197`).
   - This is the path of the failing load: `s_load_dword` at 0xd8, through `s[44:45]`, a pair built from T# words, not user data.
   - It feeds `GuestAddress` (`:201`), `ReadBda`'s lookup (`:223`), and `ReadBdaDwords`' lookup, `EmitBdaDwordReads(…, GuestAddressBase(…), mem.offset, …)` (`:216`).
2. **The emitter, user-data anchor:** `core/shader/recompiler/SpirvBackend/src/SpirvBdaRead.cpp`.
   - Each anchor now also carries `dwordOffset = (base & ~3) - begin`, emitted next to `offset` in `EmitBdaAnchorRange` (`:541`), with a new field in `SpirvEmitterState::BdaAnchor` (`SpirvEmitterState.hpp:172`).
   - `EmitAnchoredBdaRead` passes `dwordOffset` instead of `offset` to `BdaAnchorHolds` when the read is a scalar address load (`:588`; `ScalarAddressRead` at `:482`; `BdaAnchorHolds` takes the offset as a parameter, `:488`).
   - The anchored direct read therefore translates from the cleared base. Flat and global reads keep the byte offset.
   - A cleared base before the anchor's range start wraps to an offset outside the range, so it falls back to the lookup, which is case 1.
3. **The CPU twin, used by the materializer:** `ImageTableAbi::ScalarAddressDword`, `core/shader/recompiler/ImageTableAbi.hpp:132-139`.
   - It now starts from `base & ~3`.
   - The materializer reads every record behind a pointer through it, and plans the snapshot and consumed ranges from it (`ResourceMaterializer.cpp:998-999` pointer hops, `:1014` indirect descriptor dwords, `:1065` address columns).
   - Before, a pointer with low bits set made `(*address & 3u) != 0u` true there, so the record was left `Unmapped`. Now its records are read, and their ranges recorded, at the cleared address.

**Unchanged: these already clear the low bits.**

- **The `addressIsFull` path** of `GuestAddressBase` (`:182-187`): the full address's low word is the `low` it already masked.
- **`GuestAddressLow`** (`:210-214`) and the immediate masks in `GuestAddress` (`:203-206`) and `ReadBda` (`:228`).
- **The SRT evaluator** (`SrtEvaluator.cpp:232-235`) and **the SRT tape** (`SrtTape.cpp:385-388`): `base & ~3` plus the masked offset and immediate. Their `userDataUse->reads` and `readTrace` record that address.
- **The driver** has no code that computes a scalar load's address; it gets ranges from the materializer.
- **Not scalar loads, left alone:** the BVH node reads in `SpirvImageEmitter.cpp:2300`, the buffer (`s_buffer_load`) paths, and vector, flat and global loads.

**What "cleared" means here.** Each part loses its low two bits: `(base & ~3) + (offset & ~3) + (immediate & ~3)`.
- This is the rule main already applied to the offset and immediate: its own unit test expected `ScalarAddressDword(0x1000, 0x23, 0x41) == 0x1060`, where masking the sum would give 0x1064.
- It is also what the SRT walker does for all three parts.
- The debt note on V# bases says `s_buffer_load` was measured on gfx1035 to read `(base & ~3) + (offset & ~3)`.
- It differs from masking the sum only when two or more parts have low bits set. The new GPU cases keep the offsets dword-aligned, so either reading gives the same address there.

## The tests

**The reuse case, now deterministic** (5bd77d00d, `tests/execution/ImageTable.cpp:58-60`, `:2442-2446`). `RunPointerSharedVsharpTests` now places its two texel arrays at a 1024-aligned array plus 0x100 and plus 0x300.
- T#s' dword 0, `texels >> 8`, therefore ends in **01** and **11** on every platform. A `Require` checks those bits.
- The checks still compare against `FloatTexels` and `OtherFloatTexels`, which hold the same contents.
- `behindAddress()` is unchanged as the model of hardware.

**A unit-level case on the GPU** (4f77724d1, `tests/execution/BdaAnchorReads.cpp:45-48`, `:135-166`). `agc_driver_bda_anchor_reads` gains a 15-word program, assembled with llvm-mc and checked by disassembly:
- `s_load_dword s4, s[2:3], s6`
- `s_load_dword s5, s[2:3], s7`
- two `global_store_dword`s through the aligned `s[0:1]`

It runs with `s[2:3]` = the guest block plus **1, 2 and 3**, and `s6`/`s7` = 0x200/0x2f4. The loads have to return the dwords at block+0x200 and block+0x2f4.
- **Why SGPR offsets:** a user-data `s_load` with an immediate offset is folded into the SRT on the CPU and never reaches the emitter. The SGPR offsets keep these loads on the GPU's BDA path.
- **Proof that they reach it:** the existing `bda_anchor_range` check, since these two loads are the only reads of `s[2:3]`.
- **What it covers:** the anchored direct read and its lookup fallback.

**A unit-level case on the CPU** (4f77724d1, `tests/ShaderMemory.cpp:2918-2924`). `verifyScalarAddressRule`, in `agc_shader_memory` and `agc_shader_bindless`, now checks that a base with low bits 1, 2 and 3:
- reads `0x1020` for (base 0x100x, offset 0x20) and `0x1060` for (offset 0x23, immediate 0x41);
- keeps the carry rules near 2^64.

Main's assertion `ScalarAddressDword(0x1001, 0x20, 0) == 0x1021`, which encoded the old rule, is gone.

**One existing expectation changed** (4f77724d1, `tests/ShaderMemory.cpp:3022-3028`). The pointer image table case from upstream's 34525fdf7 (Celegans12, "implement image tables loaded through 64-bit pointers") set the SRT's table base to `pointer | 2`. It expected every record to be `Unmapped` with no consumed ranges, which was the old rule. It now sets `pointer | 1`, `| 2` and `| 3` in turn, and expects the same codes, snapshot ranges and consumed ranges as the aligned pointer.
