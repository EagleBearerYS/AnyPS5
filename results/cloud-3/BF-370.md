## 7. Notes

1. **The harness can tell a wrong sampler from a right one.** Records 0 and 1 carry swapped palettes (`{PointWrap, PointClamp}` and `{PointClamp, PointWrap}`), and each group needs both loop iterations. A plan that used the first lane's record for the whole wave, or the wrong palette slot, would sample texel `Width-1` where `lane` is expected, or the reverse. W+T#'s alpha uses a second texel set (`OtherFloatTexels`), so a T# that came from the SRT instead of the palette would also fail.
2. **The test returns 1 on a refusal, a fault or a wrong lane, and 0 when every lane matches.** It is a red/green probe for this task, not a test to land as is. Landing it would mean registering W, and later the variants each head plans, under its normal pass rule.
3. **The test diff below is the uncommitted change made at every head.** At 014d1e30c3 it applies as a patch. At ebaa9b1565 and c147aa8647 the file's context has moved, so the same added lines went in at the same four anchors:
   - before `#include <functional>`;
   - before `constexpr std::array<std::uint32_t, 4> PointClamp{`;
   - before `void RunPointerSamplerTests`;
   - before the `--nested-sampler` dispatch.

   The added lines are identical at all three heads (checked with a diff of the `+` lines), so no port was needed.
4. **What can't be told from his line:** the S# offset. W's chain ends with a key-buffer read indexed by the workgroup id, while his is cut before that point. The other-consumer refusals don't show in the chain at all: they are uses of the V# or S# words, not arguments of the S#. So the cut part of his chain can't show whether 0x11d049aa00 has a V# that is also a buffer (§6.2: refused on every head), a second user of an S# word (planned at #9790 and #9969, then a fault), or a T# beside the S# (planned at #9790 and #9969). The shader's own GCN around pc 0xb5f0 to 0xb600 and after would show it, and so would a run at #9969 with the §6.3 instrumentation.
