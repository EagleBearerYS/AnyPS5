# BF-059: let ResolveShaderAbi honour lazy preparation

Worker: cloud-3 (Linux, 4 cores, 15.7 GiB, lavapipe from Mesa 25.2.8). Synthetic shaders only.

**Branches** (all on our #6366's head 8cd0b37a5):

## Summary

- **The change.** Under `APS5_LAZY_SHADER_PREPARE=1`, `ResolveShaderAbi` now leaves a compute or pixel stage's ABI to the shader's first draw or dispatch, which prepares it with the ABI's state.
  - A shader that never draws stays unprepared.
  - Without the switch nothing changes.
- **Measured** at 5,000 × 50k words, anonymous memory after registration:
  - lazy: 108 MiB;
  - lazy + ABI on the base: 2,531 MiB (670 s to register);
  - lazy + ABI with the change: **139 MiB (4 s)**.
- **The first use's preparation** costs about 146 ms on top of the draw's own preparation in this harness, whose draws do not use the ABI's entries (269.5 ms mean first draw against 123.7 ms in plain lazy) per program.
- **Red/green:** the new ctest fails with the change's `ShaderRegistry.cpp` hunk reverted and passes as committed.

## 1. Reading the draw path (8cd0b37a5)

**What a draw does with `ResolveShaderAbi`'s entries.** Nothing ties a draw to them.
- `ResolveShaderAbi` (types 0 and 1) builds the ABI key from the registered state plus the title's context and primitive registers.
- It plans and prepares the stage (`PlanRegistered`, then `PrepareRegistered`), which also submits a library prebuild for a registration module.
- It appends the artifacts to `prepared.entries` and the key to `registeredAbis`.
- At a draw or dispatch, `InvocationFor` and `SourceHandleFor` build the use's own request and look through `prepared.entries` for an artifact that matches it (`PreparedShaderInvocation::TryCreate`, `MatchesPreparedShader`). On a miss they prepare one for that request (`PrepareShaderWithDiagnostics`) and append it.
- `registeredAbis` only stops a second resolution of the same ABI from preparing again (`CommittedAbi`).
- Nothing at a draw reads the ABI's context or primitive registers. The entries are a cache the draw may hit.

**What a draw does today for a lazily registered shader.**
- `RegisterShader` runs `PlanRegistered`: it validates the registers and the entry point, decodes, builds the request, then discards the plan.
- It sets `prepared.deferred`.
- The first draw finds no entry and prepares at use, without the "no artifact prepared at registration" error, because the shader is deferred.
- `ResolveShaderAbi` ignored the switch and prepared at once. On BF-054's harness, lazy + ABI matched eager: 2,530 MiB against lazy's 108 MiB.

**So the preparation can be deferred.** The ABI key, and the context and primitive registers it was resolved with, can be kept, and the preparation done at the shader's first use, with that state, before the use looks for its artifact.
- **Same entries as eager.** A used shader then gets exactly the entries eager resolution would have made, including the library prebuild for its registration module. If the draw's state differs from the ABI's, it also prepares its own, as with eager resolution.

**What stays at resolve time:**
- **`PlanRegistered` with the ABI's state.** It holds the register and entry-point checks, which lazy registration also keeps, and its plan is discarded. No other validation needs the plan.
- **The ABI key,** so a repeated resolution stays a no-op.
- **#6366's deferral** of a stage whose plan throws (`DeferPipelineStage`). Such a stage gets no deferred ABI; its first use prepares for itself, as it does on #6366.

**Not covered.** `ResolveShaderAbi` for the other shader types forwards to `ResolveGraphicsStagesAbi`, which decodes the pipeline, prepares its stages and rect-list rectangles, and prebuilds libraries. That path still prepares at resolve time.
- Deferring rectangles would need a quieter path: `DrawRectangle` logs an error for each pair it prepares at draw.
- BF-054's harness and Village's 12,252 artifacts are not known to need it. It is left for later.

## 2. The change

Branch `perf/agc-lazy-resolve-shader-abi`:
- **526600f7d** `feat(agc): count a registered shader's prepared entries`: `Driver::PreparedEntries(shader)`, the counter the test reads.
- **1bfddbc99** `perf(agc): leave a lazily registered shader's resolved ABI to its first use`, with the test.

**What 1bfddbc99 does:**
- **Resolution, under lazy preparation.** For types 0 and 1, `ResolveShaderAbi`:
  - plans the stage with the ABI's state;
  - records the key and marks the shader deferred;
  - queues a `DeferredAbi` on `PreparedShaders`: the key, the context and primitive registers, and a weak device pointer.
- **First use.** `InvocationFor` and `SourceHandleFor` take the queue, outside the snapshot's lock, when it is not empty. Each queued ABI is planned and prepared with its state, and its entries appended without duplicates.
  - A plan or preparation that fails is logged; the use then prepares for itself.
  - The common path pays only the emptiness check under the lock it already takes.
- **No transactions involved.** The queue lives on `PreparedShaders`, not `PreparedShaderState`, so a `ShaderPreparationTransaction` neither copies nor overwrites it.
- **Docs:** ARCHITECTURE's `APS5_LAZY_SHADER_PREPARE` line now says so.

## 3. Measurements (BF-054's harness, ported)

**Harness:** `tests/execution/ShaderRegistrationMemory.cpp` on `exp/bf059-measure`. It is BF-054's harness without the BF-054 prototype's counters.
- **Workload:** N = 5,000 distinct pixel programs (188 code words, about 48k SPIR-V words per variant).
- **ABI resolution:** `AgcDriverResolveShaderAbi` per shader with empty context and primitive registers, when `RESIDENCY_RESOLVE_ABI=1`.
- **Draws:** 10 % of the programs, one DCB each.
- **Conditions:** warm disk cache (populated by a cold lazy + ABI run on the base: 2,766 s), pipeline journal off, lavapipe, single runs. Process start was 61–69 MiB anonymous.

**Binaries:**
- **base** is 13243db44: #6366's head plus the harness.
- **change** is 407bb9ffa: the same plus the two commits.

| run | registration | anonymous after registration | anonymous after drawing 500 | first draw of a program: mean / median / max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lazy, base | 3.5 s | 108 MiB | 362 MiB | 128.6 / 122.8 / 243.2 ms |
| lazy + ABI, base | 670.0 s | 2,531 MiB | 2,793 MiB | 123.3 / 117.3 / 207.1 ms |
| **lazy + ABI, change** | **4.0 s** | **139 MiB** | **637 MiB** | 269.5 / 254.3 / 486.9 ms |
| lazy, change | 3.9 s | 108 MiB | 363 MiB | 123.7 / 118.3 / 200.0 ms |

File-backed memory after registration: 1,978 MiB in lazy + ABI on the base, 63 MiB in all the others.

### What the numbers say

- **Lazy + ABI with the change keeps lazy's memory at registration.**
  - **Base vs change:** −2,392 MiB (−95 %), and registration takes 4 s instead of 670 s.
  - **Change vs plain lazy:** 31 MiB more, about 6 KB per resolved shader. That is the ABI key and the queued registers, plus what discarded plans left in the allocator.
  - **Plain lazy is unchanged by the change.**
- **After drawing 500, the change holds 274 MiB more than plain lazy** (548 KB per drawn shader). Each drawn shader prepared its ABI (two variants and a registration module) and also its own entry for the draw.
- **The first use's preparation time is about 146 ms per program in this harness.** Mean first draw: 269.5 ms with the change, 123.7 ms in plain lazy, 123.3 ms in lazy + ABI on the base, where the ABI was prepared at registration.
  - **Why it adds rather than replaces.** On the base, preparing the ABI at registration did not make the first draws faster (123.3 vs 128.6 ms), so the draws do not use the ABI's entries. The harness resolves the ABI with empty context and primitive registers and draws with a different state.
  - **So here the queued preparation is work the draw does not need.** In a title whose draws match its resolved ABIs, the first use would prepare the ABI and then find its artifact, instead of preparing its own.

### A policy question for the PC

| policy | what the first use costs |
|---|---|
| the task's: prepare the queued ABI at first use (what 1bfddbc99 does) | right when the ABI matches the draws; here 146 ms and 548 KB extra per drawn shader |
| record the key and drop the queued state | lazy + ABI would behave like plain lazy: 108 / 362 MiB, 124 ms first draw. A one-line change, not made. |

Whether Village's draws match its resolved ABIs decides between them. The PC's `ReportPreparedAtUse` logs, or a counter of first uses that found their artifact among the ABI's entries, would tell.
